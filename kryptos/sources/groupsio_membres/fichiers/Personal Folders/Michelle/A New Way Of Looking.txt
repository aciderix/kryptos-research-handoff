Next step. Sanborn has always insisted on the fact that it is the 'position'
 of the mistakes that is important. Now I see why. Look at the first one in 
K1, it occurs seven letters from the end of the row. In K2 the mistake occurs
 nine letters from the end of the row. A difference of TWO. But look at the 
letters - we have K and R (the first two letters of KRYPTOS) - this is also 
important. In the new K3 we have 336 letters, divide this by four. Why four? 
Four quadrants, four question marks, four lines of morse code with extra e's - 
four seems important. Hence divide by four. This gives a row length of 84.
  Something else has been staring at me all this time. The compass! A compass
 has 360 degrees, divide this by 4 and we get 90. This figure is the length of
 our keyword, as you shall see. But whats the key? Remember the 'obvious' key 
Sanborn talked about? If we label the keyword KRYPTOS according to each letters
 position in the alphabet we get the key 0362514. Start writing down the key 
from 6251403625140362514 etc. Why start at 6? We start from here because the 
K and R have already been used (the mistakes in K1 and K2). Now we have a line
 of 90 digits. Where do we put the first letter? The hint is given as Layer Two.
 We know we have four lines of text each 84 characters long. We start at the 
second layer/line and now begin to write out the cipher text. WHO under the first
 0 (the first letter of the keyword 0=K). Then we put DVTR under the next 0, TTSS
 under the next 0. Repeat until we get to the last 0. Here we just put a single 
letter A. Why? Remember Sanborn likes patterns - this cipher involves staggered 
lines, each line is shifted by two - layer two again. Now start writing the next
 letters under the 1's but start on line three, so we have HE. Move to the next 
1 and put LMLK etc until the last 1, here we just pur R and G. Under the first 2
 put the A on the last row. Repeat and fill in the cipher. We now have
