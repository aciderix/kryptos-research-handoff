Aid for newcommers. Extracted information from articles and interviews with Sanborn and Scheidt.

1. K4 is 97 symbols long, starting after the last question mark. [1]
2. Characters 64-69 read NYPVTT. When deciphered, they read BERLIN. [1]
3. Decryption has several steps. [1]
4. Either BERLIN is not the correct order of letters, or BERLIN is not corresponding to NYPVTT directly. The letters should be repositioned to get final plain text. [1]
5. Misspellings in previous parts don't mean anything. They were added to increase difficulty. [1]
6. WW in K2 is a reference to William Webster, the former C.I.A. chief. [1]
7. There is a website to send first 10 letters of proposed solution. [1]
8. Mr. Sanborn will give more clues in November 2020. [1]
9. There are 4 messages on the sculpture. [2]
10. Before carving, Sanborn had marked certain letters that could be left out. In K2 for aesthetics he left out an X separator before the last 8 letters, that led to accidental message corruption. [2]
11. The missing X in K2 does not help in further decrypting. Sanborn was just afraid people "weren't getting the whole story". [2]

12. KRYPTOS is the keyword for some of the text on the copperplate. [3]
13. All the plain text is written in English. [3]
14. The plain text contains only a lyrical mystery. There are no more codes in it. [3]
15. Ed Scheidt, the cryptographer, helped Sanborn. He examined appropriate codes, designed structural concept and limitations. Ed does not know the message, but knows the methods.[3] [5]
16. Sanborn talked about recycled paper in the interview rather much. [3] [4] (probably to hint the palimpsest key)

17. Ed set the codes. Sanborn did not have to know mathematics of it. [6]
18. Classical cryptography (substitution and transposition) was used. [6]
19. Sanborn used pen and paper for encoding. [6]
20. In the Hirshhorn Museum, Washington D.C. there is a similar Sanborn piece, called Antipodes. The order of the letters is identical to Kryptos and the punctuation is the same. [6]
21. Ed says he used a masking technique for K4 and it is the first challenge. [6]

22. You can solve the riddle just by reading the text taken from Kryptos and posted online. [7]  (probably no "raised letters" needed)
23. Several other sculpture parts are located nearer to entrance and so have most simplistic, basic (morse) code. Further you go, more sophisticated the code is. [7]
24. K2 refers to something real, that Sanborn did. If you go to the location mentioned, you may see it. It is aesthetic, not necessary for decryption though. [7]
25. K2 may refer to "naturally magnetized lodestone by a compass rose etched in granite" [8] or "round pool with fountain pump that sends water in a circular motion" at the sculpture's base (Coriolis force) [9].
