I’m a long-time K4 enthusiast and member of the list but it’s my first time contributing except for my intro message years ago.  First of all, I’d like to say “thank you” to all of you for being such a great source of inspiration :)

I recently had some free time and tried to brute force K4 with an Enigma-machine solver.  Why you may ask?  Briefly speaking, 1) have some fun learning about Enigma 2) AFAIK no Enigma results have been posted yet 3) Enigma machines are such a great part of crypto and world history (Berlin ...) and 4) they are readily available either in real or emulator form and can also be worked out in pen&paper if one has a particular configuration in mind.  Note that I do NOT firmly believe Kryptos was encoded using Enigma and agree with those stating it is too "complex" a method, but I also believe that if any rotor device was used it would be one overwhelmingly known, and that in that case the resulting plaintext would also be readily recognizable without further processing (already enough obfuscation).

Of course, my results are not conclusive (else I would have chosen another title for my mail;)  But I’d still like to share them for general peer-review or in case someone else is also considering venturing this way or has interest for further analysis.  As for now I did nothing with the solutions except make sure none of them are plain English.

I’m describing in more details the Enigma configurations I tested below, but it’s worth mentioning that the Enigma machine evolved thorough history and I certainly did not try ALL the possible configurations.  For example, I only brute-forced plugboards with 1 or 2 cables plugged in (i.e. 2 or 4 letters), 2 cables being of common-use in the configuration books I saw from WWII.  For higher configurations I used dictionary words.  Also, different rotors were made available at different periods and I only tested what seem to be the five “standard” M3 ones.

So if you have insights on configurations that might be worth trying that I have neglected, please feel free to share them with me and I will try them out if feasible :)  I’m also of course open to share the source-code.  Actually, I am planning to send another email very soon about ways to unify such “brute force” approaches.


Ok, here's a quick overview of the technique used:

 - For all "categories" of plugboard connections (i.e. 1 cable used, 2 cables used etc.), the algo covers all combinations of using three out of the the five available rotor models, along with all possible starting letters for each rotor.

 - Of course, what I am looking for are Enigma configurations that give plaintext BERLIN at index of NYPVTT.

 - Giving credits where due, my code is strongly inspired by the one posted by Didier Stevens here: http://blog.didierstevens.com/2006/12/28/brute-forcing-enigma/.  I though re-wrote the program in C++ (warning: the C# version from the URL has serious perf issues) and added some goodies for parallel computation and such.

 - The solutions can be validated with this independent Enigma emulator: http://enigmaco.de/enigma/enigma.html

 - The solutions also check out to this other emulator http://startpad.googlecode.com/hg/labs/js/enigma/enigma-sim.html, although the “rings” parameter is not handled by my program (must remain at AAA ... one of those configurations I talked about that I did not test, I’m actually not even sure about what it parametrizes if anyone can enlighten me!)

 - Note that for the "dictionary attacks" of the plugboard, you can't use words with repeating letters for obvious reasons.  If you interpret the results below, it's worth mentioning that I did not try to find any words with more than 10 chars without repeating letters, that's why there is no such entries (it is not because I did not find any solutions, I just did not test them if any such words exist).

 - Lastly, in the results below I did not bother entering the last few chars after BERLIN, but if one particular result interests you, you can use one of the emulator listed above to get the full string.
