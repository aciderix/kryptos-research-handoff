x INTRODUCTION
Kryptos is a sculpture by Jim Sanborn, dedicated at CIA headquarters on 3 November 1990. It contains four encrypted passages. The first three — K1, K2, and K3 — have been publicly solved. K4, a
97-character ciphertext, remains methodologically unresolved in the public record after more than 35 years. In November 2025, Sanborn released final public context: the clue BERLINCLOCK refers to the
World Clock (Weltzeituhr) at Alexanderplatz in Berlin, and the K4 solution is connected to his 1986 Egypt trip and the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989. This framework proposes that K4 encodes a
navigational key — not merely plaintext — derived from the ciphertext's own structure, the solved K2 coordinate, and the public clue family EAST · NORTHEAST · BERLIN · CLOCK. The two original
solved-section keys, PALIMPSEST and ABSCISSA, are not decorative labels. They are the method: one instructs how to read, the other instructs where to look. Together they convert a sculpture into a
map, a coordinate into a viewing origin, and a set of directional words into testable great-circle bearings.
PALIMPSEST — The K1 Key: How to Read
The literal meaning
A palimpsest is a surface that has been written on more than once, with earlier text incompletely erased and still
partly visible beneath the later writing. Manuscripts, stone tablets, parchment — all were reused, each layer
partially obscuring and partially revealing what came before.
Applied to Kryptos — layers of inscription
Kryptos is itself a palimpsest. The cipher text is written over a 9x9 grid, which is written over a campus map,
which is written over a Berlin map, which is written over underground routes and concealed names. Each layer
is partly readable through the one above — exactly as the word describes. The K2 coordinate is hidden inside
the solved plaintext: written over by the cipher, visible only once K2 is decoded. The answer is already there,
beneath the surface.
Applied to the physical campus — built over
The CIA compound was literally constructed over the existing Virginia landscape — roads, buildings, and
installations placed on top of earlier geography. The Kryptos sculpture sits above a motor room 2 m
underground, connected by cable to the NHB entrance installation. The Berlin Wall monument arrived two years
after Kryptos — a later inscription placed over the existing installation. The campus is a physical palimpsest:
each new structure written over what was already there.
Applied to Berlin — the buried city
Alexanderplatz was rebuilt over Cold War rubble. The U-Bahn and S-Bahn networks run beneath it, invisible
from the surface. The WWII flak towers at Volkspark Friedrichshain are buried under a park — a landscape
written over a bunker. Operation GOLD ran entirely beneath the streets of Berlin, intercepting communications
through cables buried under Schonefeld. Berlin is a city written over its own hidden infrastructure.
ABSCISSA — The K2 Key: Where to Look
The literal meaning
The abscissa is the horizontal x-coordinate in Cartesian geometry — the distance measured from the y-axis
along a line parallel to the x-axis. In a standard coordinate system the abscissa is the baseline: the ground-level
reference line from which vertical depth is measured downward. Sanborn chose this specific mathematical term
as the K2 repeating cipher key. It is not a random word.
As a directional instruction — the east-west axis
On a north-up map the x-axis runs west to east. ABSCISSA therefore points the solver to look horizontally — not
up or down the map, but along the ground line. From the K2/W51 viewing origin, east at 44.42 degrees leads
toward Berlin and the Weltzeituhr. West at 269.94 degrees leads toward Mummy Mountain in Arizona, named
for its Egyptian-mummy silhouette — 0.056 degrees south of due west on the ABSCISSA axis. The E-back ray
at 259.36 degrees traces the Cold War east-west division of Berlin itself.
As an underground reference — depth below the baseline
In a coordinate system, the abscissa is the surface line. Everything below it is negative — depth measured
downward from the ground. The K2 plaintext says information was gathered and transmitted underground and is
buried somewhere. That information is below the abscissa. Operation GOLD operated below street level. The
Kryptos motor room is below grade. The Weltzeituhr has a room beneath it. The abscissa is the surface; the
secret is below it.
As a map coordinate — hidden inside the solved text
The K2 plaintext contains a GPS coordinate: 38 degrees 57 minutes 6.5 seconds N, 77 degrees 8 minutes 44
seconds W. This coordinate is the abscissa made literal — a specific horizontal position on the earth's surface,
embedded in the solved text, displaced approximately 150 to 170 feet southeast of the sculpture. That displaced
point, W51/K2, is where all ray tests originate. ABSCISSA named the key; the key unlocked the coordinate; the
coordinate defines the origin.
