The world of spies is a world of shadows and mirrors.  A world in which truth is 
elusive.  A world where much of the data gathered is an incomplete picture of the total 
situation, and riddled with mistakes.  This elusiveness, I believe, is one of the main 
themes of Kryptos.  Below, I report what I have found.  The deciphering is veriﬁable, 
and the process is repeatable.  But, unfortunately in this world there is no 
decipherment algorithm that tells wether or not what is reported as plaintext is the truth 
- or a lie - or a bit of both.  The decipherment and interpretations of what is found are 
mine and mine alone.  I might, inadvertently, imply that a villain is good or a hero is a 
villain.  I will do my best, but undoubtedly what you will ﬁnd below is riddled with 
mistakes.  As the Creator is the Most Merciful, the Most Forgiving, I must beg your 
pardon. 
 
One other note: please keep in mind that decipherment of Kryptos reads like a 
spy novel, ﬁlled with riddles, sudden realizations, plot twists, multiple interpretations of 
information, different parties claiming that they are telling the truth and the other is 
lying, and a signiﬁcant difﬁcultly of even knowing who is who. 
 
In this paper the following notation is used for indicating how a text is encrypted 
or decrypted.  The cipher is a Vigenere Cipher which uses the KRYPTOS alphabet as 
was used in K1 and K2:
For encryption: ENC(key, plaintext) = ciphertext
For decryption: DEC(key, ciphertext) = plaintext
Examples:
key = ELYOIE, plaintext = BERLIN, ciphertext = NYPVTT
ENC(ELYOIE, BERLIN) = NYPVTT
DEC(ELYOIE, NYPVTT) = BERLIN
For ease one could use one of the interactive keyed Vigenère solvers on the web like 
Rumkin's Keyed Vigenère Cipher at 
http://rumkin.com/tools/cipher/vigenere-keyed.php

ciphertext seem to indicate the following:

OTP, One Time Pad, Decryption Step:
 
The next step involves treating W1 as a pad and K4 as a ciphertext as part of a 
OTP, one time pad, encryption scheme.  In this scheme, one uses the pad mask as a 
'key' matched with parts of K4 as the ciphertext to recover the plaintext using the 
Kryptos Vigenère cipher, as was used in K1 and K2.
 
For each of the letters, I have tried to adopt a similar way of showing how to 
decrypt.  First I show the letter pad mask, highlighted in colors, as in Fig 1-6, the HX 
Pad Mask.  In the next ﬁgure, I show the K4 ciphertext separated out into rows and 
colored the same as the parts of the pad mask, as in Fig 1-7.  The colored part of the 
pad mask form the keys used to decrypt the the same colored part of the ciphertext.  
Next I show them all together, the keys (pad mask), followed by the corresponding 
ciphertext, and last the plaintext found after decryption.  The HX version of this can be 
seen in Fig. 1-8. I have laid it out such that one can easily use an online tool such as
