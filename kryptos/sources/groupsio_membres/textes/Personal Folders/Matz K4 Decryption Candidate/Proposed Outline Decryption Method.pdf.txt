Proposed Outline Method For 
Decryption Of Kryptos K4 
 
 
 
 
By Matt Reid 
May 2020 
 
 

[[page 2]]
Overview 
This paper puts forward what is believed to be the underlying method necessary for deciphering the 
Kryptos K4 cipher.  It is at the time of publishing believed to be an original piece of work that draws 
upon and extends the discoveries already put into the public domain by the author’s previous 
papers, ‘Valkyrie’ in 2013 and ‘The Machine’ series of long and short form papers submitted to the 
Kryptos Yahoo group in 2017.  The paper does make some attempts at the necessary puzzle steps 
themselves, but at its core there is some essential cryptanalysis that the more mathematically 
inclined may find useful in order to rule this in or out as viable framework within which the entire K4 
section of Kryptos will eventually play out.  Most importantly this method would appear to set 
parameters on what can be valid solutions.  It will also show that a final contiguous 97 letter answer 
must be protected to some degree by 4 distinct puzzle steps that would appear to pivot around the 
underlying methodology outlined in this paper.    
The overall premise is that K4 is segmented into 7 distinct X shaped layers that comprise 16 
characters, broken down into 2 distinct groupings of 8 each representing one arm of a composite X 
shape.  The 8 character groups have a distinct pattern that is built up 2 characters at a time.  Each of 
the 2 characters are a palimpsest taken directly from the cipher text itself.  The 2 characters are read 
by encrypting them against the relevant characters in the K4 cipher using the standard Quagmire III 
method and Kryptos alphabet.  Two further methods are used to obfuscate this process.  The first is 
a simple keyed Caesar shift that is applied after each successive block of 8 has been encrypted.  The 
second method of obfuscation is an alignment process that makes use of the plaintext to indicate 
the parameters that determine the shape and form for the 8 block character groupings in the cipher 
text.  Whilst this process sounds daunting, it is hoped that the visual breakdown given herein, will 
show that it is actually more consistent than it sounds from the description above. 
Limitations 
At the time of writing, the complete alignment process is still a work in progress as it will be seen 
that this part of the decryption scheme represents the actual ‘sluething’ required to find a route 
through to the final decrypt.  The proposed underlying method however generates all known 
solutions in their correct positions when combined with a brute force approach to fixing the 
positions, which then allows the alignment method to be conjectured retrospectively for each case.     
Prerequisites 
In order to successfully use the method described herein it is probably easiest to use a software tool 
that is capable of applying the following transforms to the Kryptos Cipher Text. 
 
A method of shifting individual rows to the right or left over a field of 32 characters, 
whereby a right shift will make the current 32nd character the 1st character and shift all 
subsequent character positions right by 1 position and so on. 
 
A method of transforming the cipher text in bulk by use of a keyed Caesar cipher where the 
key is KRYPTOS.  This can in fact be done in by using the decryption tableau on the sculpture 
itself, but software is recommended for expediency. 
 
A method of showing the plaintext and cipher text side by side that allows transforms such 
as row shifts to be applied and shown simultaneously.

The discoveries shared in this paper make use of bespoke software written by the author in order to 
achieve these functional requirements, but written tables are provided in order to allow access to 
the same outputs as the software. 
As can be seen here, the software gives a simultaneous view of both the plaintext and the cipher 
text.  Any positional shifts applied to a given row are made in both views. 
 
Additional features of the software also allow for easy pattern matching and the running of 
algorithms that can perform large scale scans of the PT and CT once compelling patterns have been 
identified.  The most important recent addition has been the addition of a button that can cycle the 
entire PT and CT through a keyed Caesar shift one letter to the right each time.

The Mist X 
A new algorithm was trialled in order to spot any symmetrical correlations between the first three 
rows of Kryptos in K1/K2 and the bottom three rows in K4.  The general goal of the algorithm was to 
identify groupings of 3 letters that shared similar spatial arrangements in the first 3 rows of K1/2 and 
the full three rows of K4.  The yield from such an algorithm was expected to be low given that visual 
inspection had not provided any other example other than IAC that was initially found intriguing due 
to both the specific letters involved as well as the output string that was being derived.  It was 
therefore surprising when the algorithm turned up over 2000 correlations, so it was reasoned that 
the alignment scheme must be being driven by a more restrictive process.  This led to a tightening of 
the algorithm to find only pairs that made a corresponding X pattern when overlaid.  This reduced 
the yield to about 60 candidates and some of these were explored.  Amazingly one such pair had 
been present all along in the alignment scheme already detailed and was simply overlooked due to 
cognitive bias based on the belief that the 3 letter marker would likely be CIA.

X Layer pattern alternation every 32 characters 
It will be noticed that we have skipped the B of BERL(I)N in a known section of plaintext.  It is 
reasoned that there is a change of pattern in the order in which the X pattern is read off every 32 
characters.  The plain text revealed thus far barely touches on this middle section of 32 characters, 
which is believed to be intentional as it leaves additional hurdles to overcome in the decryption 
process and further limits the potential attack surface for any algorithmic attempts at brute forcing 
the result.  It would also mean that a three row block similar to those that we have been using as X 
layers may be involved in some kind of higher level control process as each row has the potential to 
contain 32 characters.  Alternatively this may also have a basis in the 5 bit binary scheme that 
appears to be hinted at in the layout of K4 whereby rows of 26 are augmented with a 5 character 
remainder.  
Whilst investigating this middle block of 32 characters two alternate schemes were ruled out.  First 
the use of Vigenère decryption as opposed to encryption was attempted as well as an alternate 
scheme that used left shifts instead of right shifts.  Both of these gave inconsistent results.  The 
pattern that satisfies the known plain text and would make sense aesthetically is that the middle 
block of 32 characters is read as a mirror image of our existing scheme.  This means that the rules for 
the generation of the X layer are the same, but that we are purely changing the order in which the 
two characters are read off.  If we take our original scheme for both configurations as a comparison 
then we can write out the mirror image for each case.

Further Research 
Further research is now focused on refinement of the first and second crib examples as many more
