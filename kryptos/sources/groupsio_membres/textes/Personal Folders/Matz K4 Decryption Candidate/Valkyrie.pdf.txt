Introduction 
 
The following paper sets out a series of what are assumed to be novel discoveries in relation to the 
remaining unsolved cipher texts embedded in Jim Sanborn’s Kryptos and Antipodes sculptures. 
The readership is aimed primarily at those in the Kryptos K4 community who are already conversant 
with the cipher and plain texts of sections K1-K3 as well as the associated tools and techniques.  Such 
readers may wish to skip the background notes and proceed directly to the later paragraphs.   
As with sections K1, K2 and K3 anyone wishing to repeat these results for themselves needs only the 
decryption tableau, a pen and some paper (although for reasons of pragmatism a computer of some 
description may allow for faster verification of techniques and results).   
The paper puts forward the idea that K4 per se is in fact a palimpsest of K1 and K2 that is 
discoverable via a steganographic transformation of the underlying cipher text.  It is further 
hypothesised that the resulting palimpsest has had masking and substitution applied prior to final 
decryption using the Vigenère method with the formally recognised K4 cipher as its key.  The paper 
has been informally titled Valkyrie in homage to the 20th of July plot of 1944, the reasoning being that 
just like the plot, its credibility will ultimately rest on whether or not it can assume credible control 
over Berlin (the known plain text in positions 64-69 of K4).  
I’m indebted to all the hard work undertaken in both the recognized forums and micro sites that have 
sprung up along the way in support of the commonly shared goal and hope that in time this work finds 
a place in the canon of standard references cited in the final and complete decryption.  
This introduction would be incomplete without a sincere expression of gratitude to Jim Sanborn, Ed 
Scheidt and their Federal patrons for seeding the world with such a constructive curiosity.  
Lastly I’d like to thank Elonka Dunin who took the time out to cast an eye over my initial paper back 
in 2010.  Anyone unfamiliar with Kryptos may also want to visit her Kryptos site at 
http://www.elonka.com/kryptos before proceeding any further with this document.     
Apologies are given in advance for anyone with colour blindness as the colour palettes used to render the graphics contained within this 
document may cause confusion.  Please feel free to make any necessary adjustments to the colours if reproducing this work for wider 
publication.

Part I   
Background 
 
The following information on the background of the Kryptos sculpture was gathered from a range of 
internet sources. 
 
Kryptos was dedicated on November the 3rd 1990 on the grounds of the CIA headquarters in Langley 
Virginia.  The main body of the sculpture is a worked copper scroll by Artist Jim Sanborn who was 
commissioned by the CIA to create the installation for the courtyard of their headquarters.  He was 
additionally loaned the services of a retiring CIA employee by the name of Ed Scheidt who was on 
hand to provide assistance with the cryptographic elements of the piece.  
Since 1990 there have been three public acknowledgments in relation to the decryption of parts K1, 
K2 and K3. 
A civilian computer scientist named Jim Gillogly went public with a decryption of parts K1, K2 and 
K3 in 1999 having used computational analysis to arrive at the plain texts. 
After this announcement it was revealed that a CIA employee named David Stein was recognised 
within the CIA as having cracked parts K1, K2 and K3 using a pencil and paper technique in 1998.  
It was later announced in 2005 that an NSA team led by Ken Miller, Dennis McDaniels and two other 
unnamed individuals had deciphered parts K1, K2 & K3 in late 1992 using computational analysis. 
In the years since Gillogly’s announcement a mystique has continued to grow around the unsolved K4 
cipher text.  This is partly due to its resistance to yield its final secrets, but also the mass of interest 
aroused worldwide by Dan Brown’s code sleuthing novels and the growing pervasiveness of 
cryptography in modern life.  As a result there has been a mass of internet based collaboration in the 
public domain ever since, whereby common interest groups and individuals have persistently poured 
over the clues left in the plain texts of parts K1, K2 and K3 in search of possible routes into the plain 
text of K4. 
For anyone not familiar with plain texts of K1, K2 and K3 they read as follows.

Interventions post decryption of K1-K3 
 
Issues with the decryption of K2 
 
With regards the correct decryption of K2, Jim Sanborn the artist that created Kryptos personally 
intervened on April 19th 2006, contacting the group moderators in order to say, "No, that last part is 
wrong."  Elonka’s Kryptos website at www.elonka.com/kryptos relays the following about this 
incident. 
“He also indicated that there was a missing character on the sculpture, probably something that would have 
resulted in a plaintext "X" before that section. He said that he had thought that with the missing character, the 
section in question would have come out to be an unintelligible scramble. Instead, he was astounded to see that 
by sheer chance, the resulting random text had turned out to be apparently intelligible English, "ID BY ROWS", 
although that was not what was intended.” 
 
The Berlin Clue 
 
On the 21st November 2010 Jim Sanborn once again intervened, but in this instance it was to reveal a 
clue.  Sanborn indicated that a six letter string between positions 64 through to 69 of the Kryptos 
plaintext for K4 would read BERLIN once decrypted.

Part II  
Decryption Steps And Their Derivation 
 
Before commencing this section it should be noted that K4 is not a ‘block cipher’ that can be 
decrypted by a single key.  It will become evident throughout each step that the whole of the Kryptos 
sculpture represents a holistic puzzle that is only ‘eventually’ decrypted through use of the known K4 
string.  There are many facets to the puzzle, but viewed as a whole it will be seen that the aim of the 
piece is to quite literally step us through the art and history of cryptography itself.  This is done quite 
brilliantly and bears testament to the intellect and artistry of both Scheidt and Sanborn.  I suspect that 
a further study of the cipher and plain texts after this paper has been distributed will yield many more 
encryption methods, some of which may light a way to a decryption path that does not require 
foreknowledge of the BERLIN clue.   
The discovery of a null cipher in K1. 
 
I have included the following working details for extraction of the null cipher in K1for the purposes of 
completeness.  Readers from outside the Kryptos community that are not looking to reproduce the 
results may safely skip to the next heading which is ‘First Steganographic Transform’ on page 11.   
The presence of a null cipher in K1 is found by following and solving the clues given in the plain 
texts of K1 and K3.  
The first clue was found in the first half of the K1 text.  
BETWEEN SUBTLE SHADING AND THE ABSENCE OF LIGHT 
If K1 is treated as something akin to a cryptic crossword clue then the following reasoning can be 
applied.  
 
i)  Compare between 'subtle shading and absence of' (light) by writing one over the other: 
 
SUBTLE SHADING 
ABSENCE OF 
 
ii)  Remove the letters that make up LIGHT from SUBTLE SHADING to give: 
SUBESADN 
ABSENCEOF 
 
iii)  Remove common letters in each word to leave: 
USD 
CEOF 
USD is present in the cipher text of K1 whilst CEOF is present in the plain text.

Significance of the extracted information 
 
It is hypothesised that USD and CEOF are an example of ‘Keys’, the idea being that once the 
cryptographer has extracted  the correct key from a given clue that  they are then able to unlock 
otherwise hidden information from within the rest of the sculpture texts. 
To substantiate this hypothesis it will now be shown how the strings USD and CEOF in K1 can be 
used to find the entry point into the decryption process. 
To begin, let us first temporarily discard the first 13 characters, in order to give a start point of USD. 
USDJKZLDKRNSHGNFIVJYQTQUXQBQVYUVLLTREVJYQTMKYRDMFD 
Using the Vigenère scheme to decrypt this string using K4 leads to a small but significant observation.  
The ‘encryption’ rather than decryption begins with a K.  The idea that we might have to encrypt 
rather than decrypt is interesting as it is our first encounter with the idea that symmetry may play a 
part in the decryption process.  Given that we had to decrypt in order to arrive at the plain texts of K1 
and K2, it seems counter intuitive at first that we may then have to do the inverse of this process. 
Some pointers appear to be given in the form of intentional spelling mistakes, that we should consider symmetry as being an important 
aspect of the decryption process.  The spelling mistake AU has been commented on many times, but if we consider it to mean Gold then we 
know its atomic number is 79.  This is obviously a mirror image of 97(the number of characters in K4), which may indicate that we should 
be thinking about the role of symmetry when dealing with the sculpture holistically.  A number of other clues point to symmetry including 
the water feature as well as Sanborn’s decision to create the sculpture in the form of two semi-circles rather than as a cylinder.    
By referring back to the string CEOF it is possible to cite two other clues as having relevance to the 
next step.  The first of these clues is from one of the Morse Code messages left dotted around the 
sculpture.  Once resolved one particular Morse message reads, ‘SHADOW FORCES’. 
If we quite literally shade the word FORCES then we see the recurring relation of CEOF.  Admittedly 
the R and the S is harder to justify, but not everything is given to us on a plate as we shall see.  If we 
now consider K1’s enigmatic message as a whole we are reminded that ‘BETWEEN SUBTLE 
SHADING AND THE ABSENCE OF LIGHT LIES THE NUANCE OF IQLUSION’.  From the 
perspective of rendering something graphically ‘occlusion’ is the act of blocking.  There is another 
aspect of CE OF that is interest.  CF is often used as an abbreviation for ‘compare’.  By placing CE 
next to OF we are in fact comparing and finding that CF can be fashioned by occluding both two letter 
strings.  The eventual significance of this string will be found in the concluding part of the paper.   
This leaves us with the strong indication that O and C as well as E and F may share an occlusion 
relationship when shaded.  With this in mind let us examine the first four letters of K4.  These are 
found to be OBKR.  By thinking about possible occlusion relationships we can transform OBKR to 
CRYP.  Returning to our previous observation that the encryption of (the reduced form starting at 
USD) of K4 gives us an encrypted string starting with a K we can make a deductive leap and reason 
that the target we should be looking to match is in fact KRYP.     
The string required to give us an encrypted cipher text of KRYP is UNYY.  If we now search for such 
a string in the K1 cipher text then we make our first significant discovery. 
USDJKZLDKRNSHGNFIVJYQTQUXQBQVYUVLLTREVJYQTMKYRDMFD

The length between letters proceeds in the pattern 10, 9, 10.  On its own this relationship might

The line in question reads:  I MADE A TINY BREACH IN THE UPPER LEFT HAND CORNER 
Extending the pattern backwards in both K1 and a cyclically repeating K4 string of ROBKR yields:   
EMUFPHZLRFAXYUSDJKZLDKRNSHGNFIVJYQTQUXQBQVYUVLLTREVJYQTMKYRDMFD 
PUNYY encrypted using a cipher key of ROBKR gives TKRYP. 
PUNY is of course a synonym of TINY.  Our first letter in this scheme is T which may relate to 
another Morse message that reads: T IS YOUR POSITION. 
The length between letters now proceeds in the pattern 9,10,9,10.  Adding these numbers together 
yields a value of 38.  Adding an additional sequence of 9,10 would of course give us 57.  The fact that 
this particular skip sequence was capable of generating the first two coordinates or 38 and 57 degrees 
given in K2 gave a strong indication that this was a significant step in the decryption scheme.

Conclusion 
 
It has been shown that there is a potential palimpsest in K1 and K2 that with appropriate masking and 
substitution may yield the final K4 plain text when encrypted with the known K4 cipher text.  For 
every clue found in the preceding sections there are no doubt many more that remain hidden, but the 
existence of a coherent framework underlying the decryption process should dramatically reduce the 
time and scope of the algorithms needed in order to flush these out and find a complete decryption.  
There are bound to be many mistakes and wrong turns made in this paper, but I’m hopeful that the 
row addressing scheme in itself is a meaningful contribution that will feature in any complete 
decryption.

Appendix C. 
Algorithms used 
 
The algorithms used throughout were purely the standard encryption and decryption methods that 
utilised the Vigenère tableau built into the sculpture. 
New algorithms were only needed in order to ease the finding of strings in the vertical plane of the 
texts and looking for any anagrammatic relationships that may decode from one part of the text into 
another.
