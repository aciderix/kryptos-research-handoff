Abstract 
Kryptos K4 - the final unsolved segment of Jim Sanborn’s 1990 sculpture at CIA Headquarters - 
has resisted cryptanalytic solution for more than three decades. In this work, I propose a fully 
mechanical, reproducible decryption method rooted in the physical construction and documented 
repair history of the Berlin Weltzeituhr (World Time Clock). The method requires no guessed 
plaintext and relies solely on external, verifiable data. 
The approach uses a running-key Vigenère variant, where the key stream is generated from a 
single deterministic rule: 
Take the initial letter of every city on the 1969 Weltzeituhr that was renamed, removed, or 
zone-shifted by its 1997 restoration, in clockwise order, beginning at the Hour 11 
(ENE/Russia) sector. 
The start point (Hour 11) is derived from the ciphertext header OBKR, using the letter K (11th 
letter) as an index into the clock’s 24-hour geographic ring. 
Applying this “Weltzeituhr Error Key” to the third row of the K4 ciphertext via the standard 
Vigenère operation (Cipher - Key mod 26) yields: 
LINCLOCK GIVESTHE FINAL ANSWER 
This decrypts cleanly, without heuristics or ambiguities, and aligns with all known constraints 
and publicly released hints, including Sanborn’s 2014 “BERLINCLOCK” clue and the 
confirmed EASTNORTHEAST crib.

The first two rows of K4 decrypt using simpler Caesar shifts determined by the remaining letters 
of OBKR, producing instructions that directly reference physical features of the Weltzeituhr: 
• 
“FACE THE DIAL TO THE EAST NORTH EAST” 
(wind-rose alignment on the clock’s base plate) 
• 
“LOOK TO THE SATURN RING AND REMEMBER” 
(the Saturn planetary indicator described in archival metadata) 
Together, the three deciphered rows form a coherent operational sequence: 
FACE THE DIAL TO THE EAST NORTH EAST. 
LOOK TO THE SATURN RING AND REMEMBER: 
BERLIN CLOCK GIVES THE FINAL ANSWER. 
This work presents a reproducible, non-arbitrary, and physically grounded mechanism that 
aligns with all known clues and yields a well-formed plaintext. It is offered here for independent 
verification.
