Kryptos K4 Analysis 
 
 
 
Apologies if any of the following have been discussed before and I am simply rediscovering old 
insights, but I thought some of these were interesting and hopefully might contain some ideas 
and observations worth exploring. 
 
I am a big fan of the 21 column layout that many have suggested, and have been studying it for 
a while in various forms. Most of the observations in this document are centered around that 
format as well as alphabet streams in some form or another. 
 
A small introduction to both the format and the alphabet streams, is the “JKL” that appears in 
the dead center of the chart (both vertically and horizontally in the 21 column layout. The K is 
repeated both above and below the center K in the same column, and the JLK lines up perfectly 
with the standard alphabet starting from column 1. By itself this is just sort of an interesting 
pattern, but will become more interesting later looking more into alphabet streams and their 
relationship to the ciphertext (and the plaintext!)

In this post, the author describes an interesting phenomenon where duplicate plaintext letters 
appear in the ciphertext in line with the alphabet. For example LINCL -> VTTMZ 
 
https://pcsxcetrasupport3.wordpress.com/2016/08/04/my-first-deep-look-at-kryptos-k4/ 
 
I decided to experiment with different alphabet streams to see how they lined up with the 
ciphertext. I believe the above post was created before the (EAST)NORTHEAST cribs were 
disclosed. Interestingly the author’s observation not only continues into that section of the 
cipher, but if you overlay an oscillating alphabet (A to Z to A to Z, etc…) with the text, it lines up 
with both cribs perfectly in columns 4 and 8. (An almost similar pattern forms in both sections in 
columns 12 and 16 as well)

This would not be so remarkable by itself, but it continues into the BERLINCLOCK crib as well.  
 
 
 
 
 
This next chart gets a little messy, but in it you can see that: 
 
●​ In columns 4 and 8, we have TNORT lining up with VUTSR 
●​ In columns 4 and 8, we have LINCL lining up with VWXYZ 
●​ In columns 7 through 10, we have the ciphertext PRNG lining up with PONM 
●​ In columns 7 through 10, we have the ciphertext MZFP lining up with MNOP 
 
It may all be coincidence, but it does seem to reveal a consistent pattern that begins to look 
pretty non-random. It begins to look even less random when you look into the positions where 
this is occurring relative to the overall index. (The abscissa?). The mid-point of many of these 
sections seem to land at multiples of 13 or 17, both prime. 
 
If not directly tied to the structure of algorithm itself, then possibly a window into the masking 
technique.
