ABSTRACT 
Using the C2 K4 Double-Mask-OTP Hypothesis, we decoded 
Jim Sanborn’s K4 section of the sculpture Kryptos (1990). 
Modifications to known cryptography methods were adapted 
to more tactile analog approaches favorable to an artist, 
rather than a digital (computational) method used by 
cryptographers. The hints of BERLIN and CLOCK for positions 
64 through 74 were used as a crib in the decoding of K4. 
INTRODUCTION 
Previously the C2 K4 Double-Mask-OTP Hypothesis for 
solving K4 for Kyptos was proposed on July 15, 2019. After 
much trial and error, plaintext was decoded from K4 on July 
21, 2019 using one-time pad. 
MATERIAL AND METHODS 
While the concept is not difficult in theory, the arduous part 
requires many attempts of manipulating the K4 public key 
to potentially decode K4. Through reading the dinner notes 
throughout the years, it was apparent that K4 could be 
solved with pencil and paper. 
In lieu of pencil and paper, Microsoft Excel did the heavy 
lifting for the transpositions and decoding.

In order for some form of transposition to work, public key 
(K4 ciphertext) Y would have to shift 1 character forward, 
and M would have to shift back 6 characters. Due to the 
asymmetrical nature of the shift, it would be extremely 
difficult for a single columnar transposition, and likely 
there would be some form of a double transposition. 
From Jim Sanborn’s prior process, K3 was done by first 
placing it in a 42 column by 8 row matrices, rotated it by 90 
degrees clockwise and then transposed it on a 14 column 
by 24 row matrices with an addition 90-degree clockwise 
rotation. The resulting text became the cipher for K33. 
In order to select the row and columns for K3, the values 
were calculated from the factors of 336 (2, 2, 2, 6, 7) being 
2x2x2 and 6x7. For K4, the character count was 97, which 
would be a prime number. However, including the question 
mark would increase the character count to 98 with factors 
of 2, 7, and 7. Since Jim worked with a 31-column grid, it 
was unlikely he was using a 49x2 matrix, but some variant 
of 14x7.

technique that would give him flexibility to manipulate text 
without much knowledge in mathematics or cryptography.

reordered to provide a set shift, and each individual letter

columnar shift by mirroring the order in lieu of intermittent 
swaps. Without the help of the BERLIN and CLOCK hints, 
the order of the disks might not be possible. That is, a one-
time pad can be decoded to any plaintext.

DISCUSSION & ANALYSIS 
The entire approach to K4 was to determine a method in 
which Jim Sanborn, a sculptor, would be able to create an 
extremely challenging cipher without a significant amount 
of technical cryptography knowledge. Our goal was to 
reverse engineer a method where physical manipulation 
was possible in two dimensions and also three (i.e. 
Jefferson disk K4 public key). 
While the techniques are not excessively sophisticated and 
unknown, the steps to decoding K4 is extremely laborious. 
This would not be possible without the aid of Microsoft 
Excel in terms of repeatedly manipulating, recalling, and 
decoding K4 through trial and error. Unmasking the K4 
plaintext from one-time pad required some educated 
guesswork. We surmise that it is a radio transmission from 
a field operative and the corresponding response from 
headquarters. Our best translation of the masked text is 
attached in the figures at the end. 
Possible K4 Third Hint & Multiple Plaintext Outcomes 
The decoded plaintext has very few words actually 
available to be used as hints. Following Mr. Sanborn’s 
patterns, he could choose from “SEX”, “PLAY”, or “WORK” 
or maybe pairs of letters. We may never know what his next 
hint would be. It may be also possible that using OTP could 
produce multiple outcomes of the plaintext. Our best 
attempts have resulted in one possibility, had Mr. Sanborn 
changed the hints, the outcome may have been different. 
Possible Public Keys for K0-K3 
Looking back on K0 through K3, it may be possible that all 
of the sections had some sort of public key available to 
decode the message. K0 used International Morse, K1 and 
K2 uses the Quagmire III Vigenere table on the other half of 
the Kryptos panel, while K3 may have some distinct 
markings to give clues that the section is actually a double 
columnar transposition versus a substitution. If this was 
the case, then a K4 public key pattern could have very well 
been inferred. We are unsure if Mr. Sanborn left clues to 
create a Jefferson-style disk to reconfigure K4 as a public 
key, but it is possible.  
The “Berlin Clock” & K5 Segway 
While there are many public clocks in Berlin, one of the 
clocks that would best fit the description would be the 
Alexanderplatz (Urania World Clock) from the K4 plaintext. 
At the base of the clock lies a compass rose which also 
matches the compass lodestone on the CIA grounds. It may 
be possible that the magnetic heading adjustment may 
require the physical presence along with a compass for K5. 
CONCLUSION 
Kryptos remains as an analog timepiece in the constantly 
evolving digital world. We believe the original intention of 
the artist was to utilize analog methods to experience a 
fraction of the joy of cryptography (and art) from Mr. 
Sanborn and Mr. Scheidt. The K4 section was intentionally 
future-proofed against digital (computer) brute force 
approaches and required the analog (human) touch. In our 
opinion, Kryptos remains a masterpiece for its physical 
manifestation of cryptography in American history. There 
is a progression of cryptographic methods coincides with 
key historical American eras. The intrinsic energy it took to 
physically and mentally create this sculpture cannot be 
even measured. Jim Sanborn has artistically chosen an 
elegant, yet simple way of encoding K4 while having its key 
hidden in plain sight. 
While we believe our best interpretation of the decoded K4 
section is a radio conversation between a field operative 
and headquarters, we may never know if there is an 
alternative darker possibility. Regardless of the outcome, it 
would be the true intent of an artist to leave some sort of 
esoteric meanings open to interpretation. With unlimited 
mediums available to an artist, art itself is the greatest form 
of cryptography.
