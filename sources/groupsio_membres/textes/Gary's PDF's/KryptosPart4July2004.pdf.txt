The Clue Hidden in Part 3 
 
Ed Scheidt made it quite clear that part of the problem to be explored in Kryptos was the delivery 
of the “key” to an operative in the field.  Looking back at Part 3 it seems extremely likely that the 
raised Y, A, and R, along with the horizontal grouping of the five letters DYAHR were intended 
to deliver the key for Part 3.  For details on how that information provides the dimensions of the 
matrix required to solve Part 3, see KryptosCurrentState2003118.pdf in the Gary’s PDFs 
section of our Kryptos groups file section. 
 
 
Is there similar information hidden in Part 4?   
 
The top nominee for that honor goes to the KR, SO, and YP located in the upper right corner 
(shown in red below).   
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
It is unlikely that six letters from the word KRYPTOS would: 
 
1. End up grouped together in such a prominent location.  (These are in the upper right 
corner, while the clue for Part 3 was in the upper left corner). 
 
2. Each be paired with a letter that is adjacent in the word KRYPTOS.  (KR, SO, YP) 
 
3. Survive more or less intact after a substitution and/or transposition. 
 
For these reasons it appears that these letters are a clue that was added AFTER the original 
encryption of Part 4. 
 
Please note that the “T” at the beginning of the 3rd line may or may not be part of this clue.  This 
is tempting because we are only a single T short of being able to spell KRYPTOS.  And SOT are 
adjacent letters in KRYPTOS.  For now we will assume that it is there by chance and thus 
remains part of the message rather than part of the clue.  This may need to be rethought latter.

1. Looking at the letters in the order in which they appear in KRYPTOS, the KR and YP 
read left to right, while SO reads right to left.  Maybe they indicate the direction that text 
should be put in to (or taken out of) a matrix.  First row – left to right, second row – right 
to left, third row left to right. 
 
2. KRSOYP (or KRSOTYP) could be a key indicating the order in which columns need to 
be rearranged, either moving them into KRYPTOS order or, more likely, alphabetical 
order. 
 
3. Or, just as the raised letters and the grouping in Part 3 provided two clues, maybe the KR 
SO YP provides both the direction of the text and the order for manipulating the rows. 
 
 
The Cipher Text, at this point. 
 
Regardless of the meaning of the clue, the letters KR, SO, and YP are not part of the message.  So 
the text now reads: 
 
?OBUO XOGHU LBSOL IFBBW FLRVQ QPRNG 
KSTWT QSJQS SEKZZ WATJK LUDIA WINFB 
NVTTM ZFPKW GDKZX TJCDI GKUHU AUEKC AR 
 
Again, note that T (shown in red) may or may not be part of the message.   
 
 
The Substitution 
 
The substitution seems surprisingly straightforward.  While the IC (Index of Coincidence) 
produced by the text would seem to rule out a simple substitution in the English language, it 
should be kept in mind that Scheidt clearly said the message was in English and implied that 
something might have been done to hide that fact. 
 
There is a very simple technique that is used to solve Caesar ciphers – messages encrypted by 
simply shifting one alphabet relative to another.  You graph the frequency distribution of English 
on one piece of paper and the frequency distribution of the message on another and simply slide 
them relative to each other until you find a good match.  The match, when found, is usually quite 
obvious. 
 
Through a process of trial and error, using a number of obvious alphabets (straight, reversed, 
KRYPTOS in one or both, etc.), I eventually found a match that seemed just too good to be true.  
Given the small size of the sample, the match was absolutely stunning – certainly as good as you 
would expect to find in a simple Caesar of similar length.

Distribution of 91 Letters of Kryptos Part 4 Text 
 
 
The evidence that this is the correct substitution is, in my opinion, overwhelming: 
 
1. The matches in the peaks and valleys are quite dramatic – especially considering the 
small size of the sample. 
 
2. The alphabets seem quite appropriate for this puzzle and not in the least bit contrived.  
The first is simply an alphabet with the word KRYPTOS inserted right after the QU.  
While the other is a straight alphabet, slightly shifted. 
 
3. Even three of the anomalies, the QT, XE and ZF pairings add credibility to the match.  
One technique to make the text “look less like English” would be the addition of low 
frequency letters.  Q was already used as a null at the end of Part 3.  X has been used in 
previous sections as a paragraph break.  If the goal was to lower the IC, then it would 
make sense to spread the nulls among several low frequency letters.  Z would be a natural 
choice.  
 
 
Other Evidence? 
 
This is a bit more speculative, but if we were to look back at earlier sections for clues that might 
provide an operative with the information necessary to construct these alphabets we find: 
 
1. The K and R spelling errors in Parts 1 & 2.  These correspond to Q and U in the plain 
text.  In our first alphabet, KRYPTOS is inserted right after the QU.

The Text After the Substitution 
 
   
After making the substitution the text becomes: 
 
?ISUI YIABU FSNIF CZSSR ZFMKL LJMHA 
ENQRQ LNDLN NXETT ROQDE FUWCO RCHZS 
HKQQG TZJER AWETY QDVWC AEUBU OUXEV OM 
 
Keep in mind that the Q (shown in red) corresponds to the T that may or may not be part of the 
message. 
 
 
Are the Q and Z nulls deliberately paired with KRYPTOS letters? 
 
I spent a great deal of time working with transpositions that would result in the nulls all falling at 
the end of the message (with little luck, I might add). 
 
But, what would happen if the nulls had been randomly placed within the text?  The first problem 
is that the operative would have to have some tool that would let him or her determine which Q 
and/or Z was a null and which was part of the message. 
 
This is where another amazing “coincidence” occurs. 
 
 
?ISUI YIABU FSNIF CZSSR ZFMKL LJMHA 
ENQRQ LNDLN NXETT ROQDE FUWCO RCHZS 
HKQQG TZJER AWETY QDVWC AEUBU OUXEV OM 
 
 
Eight of the ten “nulls” are directly paired with letters in the word KRYPTOS.  And even the 
remaining two are adjacent to such pairs.  
 
I can imagine a “rule” for placing the nulls that goes something like this: 
 
Place each null directly adjacent to a KRYPTOS letter.  This will allow the 
operative to identify the nulls and drop them from the message.  
 
 
Following that rule, we can now drop any single null that is paired with a KRYPTOS letter.  This 
still leaves two ambiguous areas:

The first problem area – QRQ.   
 
If you dropped only one Q, which one would it be?  Should you drop both Qs?  That just doesn’t 
seem consistent.  If both were to be dropped, why not just pair up the second Q with a different 
KRYPTOS letter? 
 
There is yet another possibility.  The first Q corresponds to our mystery “T” that may or may not 
be part of the text.  If that Q wasn’t there, then there is no ambiguity in this situation.   
 
So, rethinking the original clue, let’s include the T, making the clue KR SOT YP and 
simultaneously eliminating our current problem. 
 
 
The second problem – KQQ.   
 
Well, if our rule for the placement of the nulls is correct, there does have to be a method of 
handling a natural Q or Z that appears in the text and just happens to be next to a KRYPTOS 
letter.  (Not an unlikely event considering that many of the letters in KRYPTOS have a 
reasonably high frequency). 
 
The most straightforward technique would seem to be simply placing one of the nulls next to the 
natural pair.  This way the null Q would be dropped, while the real Q remains. 
 
Note that this would require that the null Q be placed adjacent to the natural Q (as in KQQ), not 
adjacent to the KRYPTOS letter (as in QKQ).  The latter would leave an ambiguity that could 
result in either KQ or QK remaining.     
 
 
The final text – prior to the transposition. 
 
Applying these guesses to the two problem areas we are left with the following message: 
 
?ISUI YIABU FSNIF CSSRF MKLLJ MHAEN 
RLNDL NNXET TRODE FUWCO RCHSH KQGTJ 
ERAWE TYDVW CAEUB UOUXE VOM 
 
And a “key” to work with of: 
 
KR SOT YP 
 
Of course I could be wrong. ☺
