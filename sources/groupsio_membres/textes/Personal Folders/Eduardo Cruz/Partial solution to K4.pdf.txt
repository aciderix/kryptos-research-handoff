During my analysis of Kryptos K4, I've come to the strong conclusion that none of the methods or 
combination of methods previously used in K1, K2 and K3 are at play in K4. There's a few reasons as to 
why I think this is the case, the analysis and discoveries presented further below demonstrate that no 
vigenere table is used in Kryptos as seen in K1-K2, and also no usage of transposition techniques as seen 
in K3.
Instead, and as already pointed out by the creators of Kryptos publicly, K4 seems to consist of two 
cipher methods stacked together working in combination, a first layer with the purpose of masking the 
english language to complicate analysis, and a second final cipher layer seating on top. 
The biggest trace as to how K4 is ciphered comes from padding K4 to 98 letters (97+1), this arrangement 
offers the possibility of aligning K4 in rows using mathematical divisors 2, 7, 14 and 49. Out of these, 
aligning K4 to a width of 7 columns and 14 rows produces a very distinct feel with as many as 14 
observable repetition patterns, both occurring horizontally and vertically. To top it up, in such 
arrangement both pieces of known plain text happen to start precisely at the beginning of a block. The 
resulting 7x14 block structure looks as follows:

Focusing the analysis on blocks containing known plain-text (blocks #4 #5 #10 & #11), average cipher-text 
to plain-text distance reveals some very interesting and important information. 
On one hand we can see the cipher process being additive as it generally tends to push the plain-text 
alphabet up. On blocks #4 and #5 this push seems to leave things very close to shore, on average it 
pushes plain-text up by just 3.32 alphabet positions. 
On the other hand, the fact that known plaint-text block pairs share close plain-text to cipher-text 

[[page 3]]
distances, potentially indicates the same or a very similar ciphering mechanism is used on each pair. 
This hypothesis seems to be true at least for blocks #4 and #5 as we will see in a moment.  
When comparing blocks #4 & #5 vs #10 & #11, the distance factor goes up significantly to an average of 
8.32 characters  (blocks #10 & #11) vs 3.32 (blocks #4 & #5). This seems to indicate that the cipher 
process and/or its parameters are not always the same and get changed at a certain interval. 
Let's now focus solely on blocks #4 and #5, the longest and most complete K4 section of known plain-text.
K4 7-letter block:   #4       #5
Letter position:     1234567  1234567
Cipher-text:         FLRVQQP  RNGKSSO
Known Plain-text:    EASTNOR  THEAST
(the last letter of block #5 plain-text is unknown)
When looking at these two blocks, a few obvious traits can be observed:
- The "S" on the second "EAST" plain-text word matches its cipher-text counterpart. 
- "FLRV" and "GKSS" cipher-text both representing plain-text word "EAST" are at very close distance, 
mostly shift by 1. 
- Block #4 plain-text "NO" and block #5 "ST", two alphabet consecutive plain-text letter pairs that 
sharing the same block location as characters 5 & 6, both produce a similar cipher-text repetition 
pattern: "QQ" and "SS".
- Average cipher-text vs plain-text distance is very close on both blocks: 3.14 and 3.50 alphabet 
positions.
The careful consideration of these traits offers enough hints to the presence of a repeating cipher 
system, perhaps with interferences from at least one additional cipher system. The main challenge to 
solving K4 is being able to untangle the different cipher techniques into something arguably logical and 
repeatible.

Regarding the missing two letters at position #7, most likely Layer-2 shifts are 0, 1 ,2 or 3. Shifts -1 
-2 -3 -4 -5 or 4 are not possible due to collisions with other known CT-PT letter pairs. 
Shift by 1 is my favorite because its overall roundness: 
-The number is not used before in the shift series.
-It creates an incremental sequence at the odd positions (-2, -1, 0, 1). 
-In the substitution alphabet, shifting block #4 letter at position #7 by 1 gives letter "O", a -3 shift 
position from known plaintext "R", creating a correspondence with the known "O = R" seen in #4 letter 

[[page 8]]
position #6. 
-Same distance mirror happens with #5 letter position #7, shifting by 1 gives letter "N" also at -3 from 
alphabet letter "Q", also a correspondence with "N = Q" seen in block #4 letter position #5. 
-If the last letter of block #5 were "Q", the resulting word could be QUADRANT or QUARTER, terminology 
compatible with compass directions and mapping, a great fit with the preceding sentence "EAST NORTHEAST".

I hope this solution to the EASTNORTHEAST passage helps cracking the remaining of K4, my attempts to 
untangle blocks #10 & #11 of known plaintext have failed to produce any viable results due to a lack of 
usable coincidences. A few strong clues indicate blocks #10 & #11 ciphers unlikely to share parameters 
with blocks #4 & #5, and also not among themselves. Here's a few reasons:
-Using blocks #4 & #5 Layer-2 parameters on block #10 creates a "T" collision on letters #4 & #5, hence 
block #10 doesn't seem to use the known layer-2 parameters.
-On Block #11 letter #4 both the cipher-text and plaintext is letter "K", this outcome is not posible by 
using the known cipher parameters from Block #4 & #5.
-Block #10 and #11 position #3 cipher-text is both "P" while the expected plaintext is "R" and "C", this 
collision wouldn't happen if the blocks shared the same cipher parameters.
-Block #10 plaintext "E" and Block #11 "O" share the same position #2, assuming Layer-2 position shift is 
the same for both letters, if Layer-1 substitution was the same as seen in blocks #4 & #5 these should 
show at an equal distance vs their correspondences. In blocks #4 & #5 once Layer-2 is solved, known "E" 
and "O" are at an equal distance of 3 from their Layer-1 cipher correspondences "H" and "R". 
No other K4 blocks seem to follow the cipher/parameter mechanics of blocks #4 & #5. Analysis of blocks #3 
and #6 produces favorable Layer2 results but nothing conclusive, no english language is produced when 
applying the known layer1 parameters.
