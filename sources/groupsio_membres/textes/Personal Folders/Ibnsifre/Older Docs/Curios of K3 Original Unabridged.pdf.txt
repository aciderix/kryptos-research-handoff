To The Kryptos Yahoo Users Group, 
     I'm aware that many have been searching for a solution to the K4 section.  However, have you ever wondered 
about K3.   What is it about?  How does it relate to the first two sections?  It's about finding a long lost tomb. 
 What does that have to do with Intelligence, Communications, Information Gathering, or any other discipline of 
spycraft? 
Maybe Nothing.
    In the first two sections, K1 and K2, Jim Sanborn, Kyptos' creator, used a Vigenère cipher to encrypt their 
messages.  Those required some amount of skill to decipher.  However, the third section, K3, was encoded using 
letter transposition.  Although they can be a bit maddening to work out, it was a much simpler technique.
    A Vigenère cipher's weakness is correlated to the length and predictability of its key word.  The longer and less 
predictable the key word, the harder it is to break the cipher.  What would make the best key?  It would be a key at 
least the same length as the message it encodes, and it would be chosen to be no more predicable than any other 
key its length - that is, chosen in a completely random way.
    When using a key of this type, the cipher is known as a "one-time pad".  As long as the key is kept secret by its 
users and only used to encrypt a single message, the ciphertext cannot be cracked.
    What if K4 is encrypted using a one-time pad?  As I remember, the builders of the pyramids of Ancient Egypt 
would build two chambers.  One path, the direct one, would lead to a false chamber containing nothing of value as 
to deceive the tomb raiders.  The other, off to the side from the direct one, would lead to the pharaoh's resting place 
- and to his treasure.
   What if the decipherment of K3 is really a false chamber - and the key to K4 is hidden in the ciphertext of K3?
 
- Ibn Şifre
