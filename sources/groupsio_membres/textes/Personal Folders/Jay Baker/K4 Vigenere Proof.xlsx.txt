 P A L I M S E T Z K R Y O B C D F G H J N Q U V W X ● Notice in this encryption of K1 that 'K' is the first letter of the keyworded alphabet. The second keyword, PALIMPSEST, does not contain the letter 'K'. Therefore, no instances occur where any letter, after being encrypted, translates to the same letter. However, when any letter in the second keyword (highlighted in yellow) is replaced with the letter 'K', it creates multiple instances of letters being encrypted to the same letter. For instance, in this example, the ninth letter of palimpsest was replaced with the letter K. Each and every time the 'K' was used to encrypt the message, a doublet occurred. Because the 'k' in 'clock' encrypts to 'k' means that the first letter of the first keyword is contained in the second keyword (although we don't know what that letter is). Because no other occurrence of this happens in the eleven letter hint, the second keyword can be no shorter than 11 letters.
