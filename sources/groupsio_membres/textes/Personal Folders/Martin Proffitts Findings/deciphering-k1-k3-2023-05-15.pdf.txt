Deciphering the plain text K1 to K3
This document relates specifically to my current thinking that all the clues for deci-
phering the fourth cryptographic panel from the KRYPTOS sculpture, installed at the
CIA headquarters in Langley, Virginia, come from passages 1 to 3.
Whilst it has been stated many times that Kryptos can be broken using only the K4
cipher text, for 33 years, this has been proven to be untrue as the method used to
encrypt the panel has never been discovered.
My thinking here is that the method is embedded and explained in panels 1 to 3 and
this is my journey of discovery.
Before I move on, for those reading from the Kryptos forum, you may note that
my work recently was around the discovery of primes and the speculation of an
advanced cipher and key exchange such as Diffie Hellman with Elgamal, before I
suddenly switched, almost without warning to examining the plain text in great
detail.
In case you’re wondering, this was the result of a chain of events that made me
question everything I was doing.
1. I was discussing hobbies with a colleague and our conversation turned to
adventure game books.
2. Josh LeVeque posted about media quotes related to Kryptos. https://kryp
tos.groups.io/g/main/message/23792 This made me realise just how much
noise had built up over the years.
3. Rob Mccleary posted about the Greek word ASTATOS which made me realise
I’d been totally wrong about LAQUEO, a keyword I’d based much of my
previous research around.
4. Elonka Dunin posted a statement which made me realise just how little we
understood about the plain text in K1 to K3.
5. I took a day trip to Vilanova i la Geltru on the Catalonia coast. On the way
back the train broke down and I found myself staring out to sea through a
small hole in the cliff face (a tiny breach if you will) whilst thinking about
the shape of those 97 characters.
This chain of events then started to come together in my head and brought me

Keywords
Another trap to fall into is the belief of keywords being discovered inside either the
cipher text or the plain text, especially inside K4.

bution does not match the standard character distribution, it is only 3 characters less
than the number of pieces in a standard English language scrabble game.
The number of possible ways one can rearrange these 97 characters, allowing for
substitution where relevant to form new words is approximately 2697 ≈1.31 × 10143
