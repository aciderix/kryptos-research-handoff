Introduction 
 
The following document sets out a series of what are assumed to be novel discoveries in relation to 
the remaining unsolved cipher texts embedded in Jim Sanborn’s Kryptos and Antipodes sculptures. 
Whereas this does not purport to be a complete solution it does offer a candidate for the decryption 
of K4 per se and is hoped in time to mark a significant waypoint on the road to the eventual 
discovery of the outstanding plain text.  The various statements of what are thought to be novel 
discoveries have been grouped and presented according to their viability.   
The readership is aimed primarily at those in the Kryptos K4 community who are already conversant 
with the cipher and plain texts of sections K1-K3 as well as the associated tools and techniques.  
Such readers may wish to skip the background notes and proceed directly to the later paragraphs.   
As with sections K1, K2 and K3 anyone wishing to repeat these results for themselves needs only the 
decryption tableau, a pen and some paper. 
I’m indebted to all the hard work undertaken in both the recognized forums and micro sites that 
have sprung up along the way in support of the commonly shared goal and hope that in time this 
work finds a place in the canon of standard references used to offer a complete decryption.  Should 
any aspects of the work infringe anyone’s prior art then my sincere apologies are offered as I have 
been as exhaustive as possible in searching the public domain for blogs or micro sites venturing a 
similar solution. 
This introduction would be incomplete without a sincere expression of gratitude to Jim Sanborn, Ed

Background 
 
The following information on the background of the Kryptos sculpture was gathered from a range of 
internet sources. 
Kryptos was dedicated on November the 3rd 1990 on the grounds of the CIA headquarters in Langley 
Virginia.  The main body of the sculpture is a worked copper scroll by Artist Jim Sanborn who was 
commissioned by the CIA to create the installation for the courtyard of their headquarters.  He was 
additionally loaned the services of a retiring CIA employee by the name of Ed Scheidt who was on 
hand to provide assistance with the cryptographic elements of the piece.  
Since 1990 there have been three public acknowledgments in relation to the decryption of parts K1, 
K2 and K3. 
A civilian computer scientist named Jim Gillogly went public with a decryption of parts K1, K2 and K3 
in 1999 having used computational analysis to arrive at the plain texts. 
After this announcement it was revealed that a CIA employee named David Stein was recognised 
within the CIA as having cracked parts K1, K2 and K3 using a pencil and paper technique in 1998.  
It was later announced in 2005 that an NSA team led by Ken Miller, Dennis McDaniels and two other 
unnamed individuals had deciphered parts K1, K2 & K3 in late 1992 using computational analysis. 
In the years since Gillogly’s announcement a mystique has continued to grow around the unsolved 
K4 cipher text.  This is partly due to its resistance to yield its final secrets, but also the mass of 
interest aroused worldwide by Dan Brown’s code sleuthing novels and the growing pervasiveness of 
cryptography in modern life.  As a result there has been a mass of internet based collaboration in the 
public domain ever since, whereby common interest groups and individuals have persistently poured 
over the clues left in the plain texts of parts K1, K2 and K3 in search of possible routes into the plain 
text of K4.

Initial K4 Analysis 
 
It is often said that frequency analysis is the strongest weapon in the cryptographer’s armoury and 
the identification of a route into K4 has proven to be no exception to this general rule.   Of all the 
potential ideas that were publicised in the online community the possibility of K4 being a ‘one time 
pad’ of itself is the idea that the Author believes has always carried the most weight.  As a result of 
this conviction the majority of the initial research centred heavily on such a decryption scheme.    
After multiple failed attempts and various blind detours later, it has eventually been possible to 
arrive at a credible decryption scheme, although this is not as grandiose a claim as it first appears for 
reasons that will become clear later.   
The decryption scheme employs a simple technique whereby the K4 cipher text is split symmetrically 
and laid over a reversal of itself.  In this configuration the reversed second half of the cipher text 
plays the role of the keyword.  Using the Kryptos Tableau to decrypt yields the following results.

Further Analysis Of K4d  
 
It is immediately obvious that K4d is not readable plaintext, but a number of structural factors do 
stand out immediately.  Of interest is the positional relationship information for the characters X and 
Y.  These were marked as being of interest due to the Cartesian flavour of the K2 decryption 
keyword ABSCISSA.  
If every seventh letter in K4d is skipped then it is seen that the resultant text reads off as YYYXYQX 
which to the eye looking for Cartesian linkages stands out immediately as a possible aspect of some 
underlying structural artefact. 
It is worth pointing out at this juncture that all of the discoveries that follow were not immediately 
obvious despite being the key to all the doors that followed.   The line of enquiry that the specific Y, X 
and Q combination prompted were all eventual dead-ends that explored a range of different 
encryption schemes including morse and pseudo cursor operations. 
Notes On Computational Analysis 
 
It is interesting to note in hindsight that computational analysis does not offer any major help from 
this point in as the text starts to descend in a series of structural operations and cryptic clues that 
would wrong foot any potential pattern matching software.  Combined with the disparity in the 
popularity of cryptic crosswords in America as opposed to Britain where they are pervasive it does 
not surprise me that there has been massive asymmetry in the time taken to find a route into K4. 
The Forensics Of Kryptos 
 
From investigations to date on what lies beyond this point, it is also possible to venture a guess that 
the cipher texts of K2 and K3 were laid down very early on in the project and that K1 and K4 were 
produced in tandem with each other, with the wording of the K1 plaintext being significantly 
reworked until the desired result was achieved.  The idea that K2 and K3 were already done and 
dusted combined with the amount of effort required to generate the desired effect for the K1 & K4 
combination may well have been a factor in the ‘aesthetic mistake’ inherent in K2.

The following eight statements are now offered up for critique and the corresponding analysis 
presented in more detail in the following sections.  The ninth and tenth unqualified statements 
should be disregarded as tenuous, but note taken that a meaningful plaintext may be compressed 
into 44 characters.   
1. The decryption of K4 per se is: ?YRKDKYWCBFMXYPEGCGMYJECYJOXRPPJYGYAIMNGCQREQOTBX (K4d) 
2. K4d interacts with both the K1 cipher and plain texts to reveal further structural information.  
3. K4d is subdivided into 3 further components K4d-1, K4d-2 and K4d-3 that in their entirety 
comprise 44 characters. 
 
4. Each unique section just like the four sections of the complete cipher text as a whole has its 
own distinct method of encryption that must be broken. 
5. It is ventured that the first layer decryption of K4d-1 is LCOXWHXMAKE. 
6.  K4d-1, K4d-2 & K4d-3 are each DOUBLY encrypted. 
7. Once the first layer of decryption is removed from each of the three parts, the final plaintext 
is recovered through the use of two keywords of 5 and 6 characters in length respectively. 
These are applied repeatedly four times against the underlying 44 character string to recover 
the eventual plaintext. 
8. It is ventured that the first keyword in this 5 and 6 character combination is QUXQB which 
corresponds to the K1 cipher text for the word LIGHT.   
 
9. It is tenuously ventured that the second keyword in plain text may either be HIOOGH where 
OO represents  underlying characters that should remain unchanged or GNOMON which can 
be derived tenuously from the same original source. 
10. It is conjectured that the first five letters of the K4 plain text proper are WHATI and an 
unqualified guess ventured that this may well correspond to a quote by Andre Malraux. 
High confidence is placed in statements 1 – 3, but evidence supporting statements 4 -8 is based solely 
on work undertaken against K4d-1 and is deemed speculative until further supporting evidence is 
found.

Statement 5.  It is ventured that the first layer decryption of K4d-1 is 
LCOXWHXMAKE 
 
By using K4d Fragment 1 as the cipher text and K1C Fragment 1 as the corresponding keyword to

LCOXWHXMAKE.   
The discovery of this plaintext was thought at the time to be significant as it might imply an entire

the various strategic bodies at the time of the Cuban missile crisis as any resultant plain texts might 
be obscured by intelligence specific grammar’s that appear meaningless in plain English .  Examples 
include ALCC, SAC & ICBM etc. 
Statement 6.  K4d-1, K4d-2 & K4d-3 are each DOUBLY encrypted. 
 
Initial thoughts on the decryption of K4d-1 being the true plain text are now thought to be over

schemes for K4d-2 which so far have yielded no meaningful results.

 Claim 10.  It is conjectured that the first five letters of the K4 plaintext

obscure’.  What is effectively a social hack led to a brief search for potential quotes that would fit 
such criteria.   
The quote ‘WHAT IS A MAN? A MISERABLE LITTLE PILE OF SECRETS’  attributed to Andre Malraux 
would appear a fitting conclusion given the nature of the work undertaken at the CIA.  Malraux 
would also fit as a potential source known to Sanborn as he wrote widely on a number of subjects 
including art and sculpture. 
Compressed into 44 characters it is conjectured that such a plain text would read something like: 
WHATISAMANXAXMISERABLELITTLEXPILEXOFXSECRETS  
Should anyone search the internet in pursuit of a source for this quote, then they will no doubt come 
across multiple references from its use in a video game published by KONAMI in the 1990’s.  In the 
time since the sculpture was installed the game became a hit, and its obscure dialogues passed into 
the vocabulary of teen sub culture.  As a result there are multiple sites on the internet where T-shirts 
or mugs emblazoned with the same wording may be purchased.  Given that the final plain text may 
not be revealed for some time to come the Author is happy to settle on this conclusion as he notes 
with a wry smile that teenagers all over the western world may have been walking around the 
streets keeping the secret in plain sight for most of the last decade.
