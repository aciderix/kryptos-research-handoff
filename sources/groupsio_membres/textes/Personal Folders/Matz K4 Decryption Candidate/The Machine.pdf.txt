Introduction and Thanks 
 
The following paper is essentially a more detailed retelling of the previous paper entitled ‘Valkyrie’ 
that has been languishing in the file uploads area of the Yahoo Kryptos Group since October 2013.  
The original paper wasn’t widely publicised as it was a place holder, summarizing discoveries to that 
date that fell well short of any meaningful conclusions about the possible final methods of decryption.  
That paper also omitted any mention of the holistic nature of the sculpture itself.  Since that time we 
have had another clue revealed by Jim Sanborn by way of the word CLOCK for positions 70 through to 
74.  Serendipity has also played a part in that intervening period in having discovered Carl Sagan’s 
novel ‘Contact’ over the 2013 Christmas holiday season.  I have amended the original paper to show 
how the book is an essential resource that directs us to lines of enquiry beyond the clues given in 
parts K1-K3.  This paper has been named as ‘The Machine’ in order to pay homage to the concept in 
Sagan’s Contact novel that once ‘the message’ was decoded, that it laid bare instructions for how to 
build a machine to reconnect mankind to the cosmos. 
As usual the readership is aimed primarily at those in the Kryptos K4 community who are already 
conversant with the cipher and plain texts of sections K1-K3 as well as the associated tools and 
techniques for the relevant decryptions.  This is a lengthy paper, but no apologies are given for that 
other than for it being perhaps too brief in places, but I have endeavoured to include enough detail to 
make the reconstruction of the results as clear as possible.  There will no doubt be some mistakes and 
some areas where I could have expanded more with clearer examples and diagrams, but the general 
premise presented herein is sound and self-consistent, so please feel free to copy any material in this 
paper in order to illustrate any corrections as part of your own work.  I’m happy for this work to be 
redistributed in its entirety or drawn upon in parts, but please include a citation.  I have not submitted 
this paper to Jim Sanborn for verification as nature ultimately completes that particular job for us. 
As with sections K1, K2 and K3 anyone wishing to repeat these results for themselves needs only the 
decryption tableau, a pen and some paper (although a computer and relevant software is 
recommended).  Source code is provided (with no copyright or warranty, so use at your own risk) in 
the appendix for people looking to reconstruct ‘The Machine’ and play around with different 
decryption algorithms.  The K3 decryption algorithm is included out of the box as part of this source 
code, but as the eclipse of 21st August 2017 looms before us I’m hopeful this paper may inspire many 
newcomers to take up the challenge to find a way to use Kryptos to predict this event as an exposition 
of its underlying function.  The rewards of such an endeavour may be to extend the findings of this 
paper even further, but for my part I’m satisfied that the results contained herein will at last break the 
long hiatus since K3 was publicly decoded.  
This introduction would be incomplete without a sincere expression of gratitude to Jim Sanborn, Ed 
Scheidt, their patrons and any other unnamed parties behind the scenes for seeding the world with 
such a constructive and educational curiosity.    
Lastly I’d like to thank Elonka Dunin who took the time out to cast an eye over my initial paper back in 
2010.  Anyone unfamiliar with Kryptos that wants additional background may also want to visit her 
Kryptos site at http://www.elonka.com/kryptos before proceeding any further with this document.

Abstract 
 
A number of what are thought to be novel discoveries are hereby put into the public domain for wider 
discussion and verification.  The core finding is that the Kryptos cipher text is layered on top of a 
numerical surface that is independent of the lettering itself.  This surface represents a hardware 
model on which to perform an analogue computation of the moons cycles in relation to the solar day 
and year.  As a result of this it is conjectured that the sculpture is in fact a symbolic representation of 
the Antikythera mechanism.  It will be shown that the sculpture is capable of recording time at the 
level of seconds, minutes, hours, days and weeks.  It can further be used to capture the calendar, 
sidereal and synodic months.  The largest time span it records is the 76 year Callippic cycle, which is 
composed of 4 Metonic cycles each of 19 years in length.  The ability to record these cycles would 
also imply that it has the ability to accurately predict eclipse cycles.  Strong indications are also given 
that additional problems will need to be solved in order to fully decrypt K4/K5 that relate to data 
manipulation and the discovery of the correct algorithmic process for letter selection.  Though there 
are many new insights in this paper the most important is that of putting a new perspective on the 
sculpture in that it is far more than a redundant copper scroll once the K4/K5 plaintext is known and 
reveals instead a new function that takes it into perpetuity.

For anyone unfamiliar with the novel ‘Contact’ by Carl Sagan, I would suggest picking up a copy, not

Part I 
Background 
 
The following information on the background of the Kryptos sculpture was gathered from a range of 
internet sources. 
Kryptos was dedicated on November the 3rd 1990 on the grounds of the CIA headquarters in Langley 
Virginia.  The main body of the sculpture is a worked copper scroll by the Artist Jim Sanborn who was 
commissioned by the CIA to create a series of installation pieces for the buildings courtyard.  He was 
additionally loaned the services of a retiring CIA cryptographic expert by the name of Ed Scheidt who 
was on hand to provide assistance with the cryptographic elements of the underlying text.  
Since 1990 there have been three public acknowledgments in relation to the decryption of parts K1, 
K2 and K3. 
A civilian computer scientist named Jim Gillogly went public with a decryption of parts K1, K2 and K3 in 
1999 having used computational analysis to arrive at the plain texts. 
After this announcement it was revealed that a CIA employee named David Stein was recognised 
within the CIA as having cracked parts K1, K2 and K3 using a pencil and paper technique in 1998.  
It was later announced in 2005 that an NSA team led by Ken Miller, Dennis McDaniels and two other 
unnamed individuals had deciphered parts K1, K2 & K3 in late 1992 using computational analysis. 
In the years since Gillogly’s announcement a mystique has continued to grow around the unsolved K4 
cipher text.  This is partly due to its resistance to yield its final secrets, but also the mass of interest 
aroused worldwide by Dan Brown’s code sleuthing novels and the growing pervasiveness of 
cryptography in modern life.  As a result there has been a mass of internet based collaboration in the 
public domain ever since, whereby common interest groups and individuals have persistently poured 
over the clues left in the plain texts of parts K1, K2 and K3 in search of possible breaks. 
For anyone not familiar with the layout of the sculptures original cipher text or the plain texts of K1, 
K2 and K3 then they read as follows.

Interventions post decryption of K1-K3 
 
Issues with the decryption of K2 
 
With regards the correct decryption of K2, Jim Sanborn the artist that created Kryptos 
intervened personally on April 19th 2006, contacting the group moderators in order to say, 
"No, that last part is wrong."  Elonka’s Kryptos website at www.elonka.com/kryptos relays 
the following about this incident. 
“He also indicated that there was a missing character on the sculpture, probably something 
that would have resulted in a plaintext "X" before that section. He said that he had thought 
that with the missing character, the section in question would have come out to be an 
unintelligible scramble. Instead, he was astounded to see that by sheer chance, the resulting 
random text had turned out to be apparently intelligible English, "ID BY ROWS", although 
that was not what was intended.” 
The Berlin Clue 
 
On the 21st November 2010 Jim Sanborn once again intervened, but in this instance it was to 
reveal a clue.  Sanborn indicated that a six letter string between positions 64 through to 69 
of the Kryptos plaintext for K4 would read BERLIN once decrypted. 
The Clock Clue 
 
On the 20th November 2014, Jim Sanborn revealed another clue in the form of the word 
CLOCK being the plaintext for positions 70 through to 74.  The fact that 11 letters of the 
plaintext were now known now massively reduced the odds of being able to find suitable 
decryption schemes.

Part II  
 
It will become evident throughout each step that the whole of the Kryptos sculpture 
represents a larger body of work than previously realised and is only eventually seen for 
what it is by solving multiple smaller puzzles.  Some of these steps are more significant than 
others, but in total they add up to a single conclusion that is both irrefutable and 
intellectually satisfying.  Viewed as a whole it will be seen that the piece quite literally steps 
us through the art and history of cryptography itself.  This is done quite brilliantly and bears 
testament to the intellect and artistry of both Scheidt and Sanborn.  I suspect that a further 
study of the cipher and plain texts after this paper has been distributed will yield many more 
encryption methods, some of which may light a way to a decryption path that does not 
require foreknowledge of the BERLIN and CLOCK clues respectively.

Examination of the plaintext for hints 
 
Like all encryption schemes, K1, K2 and K3 have weaknesses that made them vulnerable to 
cryptographic analysis and attack.  K4 has so far resisted any form of conventional 
cryptographic attack, which implies the sculpture text taken at face value is either not a 
computable quantity or if it is then uses an encryption scheme that leaves no statistical 
artefacts in its wake that can be exploited by the cryptographer in order to reconstruct the 
underlying scheme.   
The alternative is that we have to manipulate the sculpture text in some way in order to 
make it a computable surface and that K4 somehow interacts with this, rather than being a 
distinct cipher in its own right.   
The necessary manipulations that we need to make in order to make the sculpture 
computable are given by a series of clues present in the plain text of K1, K2 and K3. 
There are multiple ‘lucky breaks’ that can be made by analysing different clues in the 
plaintext, but significantly any of these initial entry points lead to the same eventual 
conclusion.  To illustrate this point I’ll start with a three part clue in K3 that is initially cryptic, 
but can then be asserted mathematically to arrive at our first meaningful break. 
I MADE A TINY BREACH IN THE UPPER LEFT HAND CORNER AND THEN WIDENING THE HOLE A 
LITTLE I INSERTED THE CANDLE AND PEERED IN THE HOT AIR ESCAPING FROM THE CHAMBER 
We can approach two aspects of this clue cryptically and one at face value.  The HOT AIR 
ESCAPING can be thought of as the FUME’s leaving the previously sealed chamber.  As we’re 
looking at the puzzle through this same cryptic lens we can also assert that the TINY breach 
might alternatively be a synonym of TINY in the form of the word PUNY.  Lastly the location of 
this breach is mentioned as being in the top left hand corner.  Given that the keyword of K1 is 
known to be PALIMPSEST it is not unreasonable to look at the top left corner of the sculpture 
text.  Combining all these factors lets re-examine the first two rows of cipher text for K1. 
E M U F P H Z L R F A X Y U S D J K Z L D K R N S H G N F I V J  
Y Q T Q U X Q B Q V Y U V L L T R E V J Y Q T M K Y R D M F D  
 
 
We can clearly see the word FUME written backwards before an alternating sequence of 9 
and 10 letter skips give us a resulting string of PUNYY.  This string is particularly interesting as 
using the exact same Quagmire III technique as used for the decryptions of K1 and K2, the 
encryption (note, not the decryption!) of UNYY with the first four letters of K4 (which are 
OBKR) gives the encrypted text KRYP.  Something that was initially overlooked is that the first 
P is attached to a word that is written backward.  The last letter of K4 is R, so if we imagine K4 
as being a repeating cycle rather than a fixed 97 letter string then this would be the first

Steganography 
Before embarking on this next section we need to clarify an important point.  The grid 
system that the Kryptos letters have been put into is an abstraction, just as geometry is an 
abstraction of the real world.  The individual blocks making the grid have a number value 
just like coordinates on an X and Y axis.  To give an example, three grid blocks means the 
number three irrespective of the letters that may be in each box.  I make this point as we 
have already made a manipulation of the text, but this has no bearing on the steganography 
that has been used in Kryptos, so for clarity I shall show the number surface using the 
original layout rather than with the re-arrangement of the first row used in the previous 
section.   
This abstraction is a powerful one as we shall see that the numerical values associated with 
the grid system can be thought of as an analogue of the modern day hardware model.  The 
letters themselves can be seen as being data that fill the registers of this hardware and our 
decryption technique can be thought of as an algorithm that is run using the underlying 
mechanics of this machine.  This may sound confusing at first given that Kryptos is purely a 
series of letters printed on copper, but once explained, the underlying genius of the 
sculpture layout will become evident.

The Coordinates 
 
The coordinates of the sculpture itself as given in K2 are quite remarkable.  The bigger 
question that will no doubt be asked post this paper will be, which came first, the 
coordinates or the CIA, by which I mean, is this a coincidence or was the siting of the original 
building itself chosen on the basis of these coordinates?  Either way it should remind us that 
there is an inherent human quality of constantly seeking out the underlying beauty of 
mathematical relationships in nature.  This appears to have existed in all cultures 
throughout all history and remains a fundamental aspect of our higher selves that seems to 
persist even through the darkest ages of ignorance.   
Returning to the discovery made at the end of the last section with regards the relevance of 
the hours and minutes.  If we take the anagram located in row 15 at its word and only 
concentrate on the hours and minutes of the K2 coordinates then we end up with the 
following relationship when summing the numbers. 
38 + 57 + 77+ 8 = 180 
We can see that hours and minutes were considered equivalent in arriving at this result, so 
let’s try the same method, but now add the seconds as well.  We’ll treat each individual 
component as a discrete number even if it is fractional. 
180 + 6 + 5 + 44 = 235 
This result is absolutely key as 235 is the number of synodic months in what is known as the 
Metonic cycle.  Once we know that there may be a lunar connection to Kryptos we can start 
reappraising other aspects of what we already know.  Firstly we know that two complete 
cycles (or traversal of the embedded coordinate system) of the Hours and Minutes will 
symbolically denote the traversing of 360 degrees.

It’s at this point that we reach our first ‘jump point’ whereby we can make a deductive leap 
and arrive at a profound result.  The result is proof that there is most likely a hardware 
model in play designed to be an integral part of the decryption process.  A quick note before 
proceeding is that the coordinate traversal requires revisiting rows 1 and 2 after we have 
reached 38.  They are then traversed a second time before the traversal of row 3 giving a 
count of 57.  I point this out to show that there are indeed 360 squares in the coordinate 
row traversal.   
The Leap 
If for every 180 degree pass through the Hours and Minutes coordinate model we accrue an 
extra 6.5 degrees (as defined symbolically by the last block of 12 characters in the first set of 
coordinates) then we eventually accrue a total of 13 degrees extra for each 360 traversal.  
This is significant as 13 degrees represents the apparent motion of the moon through the 
sky each day.  If we use the 28 spaces in the calendar block to record each time that we 
traverse 13 degrees then we get a pretty accurate method of deriving the 13 lunar months 
of the year.  We know from our calculation of the figure 235 above that we are being asked 
to be flexible about the change of units from hours and minutes to pure degrees, so it is not 
a great leap to suppose that the same is true for the seconds.  In this case once we have 
traversed the coordinate blocks for the Hours and Minutes corresponding to 180 squares we 
can switch to the block of 12 characters that symbolises 6.5 degrees.  We can choose to 
either ignore the ? symbol or not, but if we do ignore it then every time the cycle repeats we 
will have skipped forward 192 characters. 
This skip sequence will be immediately recognisable as the algorithm required to extract K3.  
All we have to do is set a counter off in K3 that traverses 1 square for every square in the 
coordinate traversal plus the squares in the lunar adjustment register.  At the point the cycle 
repeats we should record each letter currently under the K3 traversal counter.  If the cycle 
was left to run indefinitely then we would eventually end up at a period of 19 years at which 
we would have an integer correlation between the number of sidereal moon months and 
the number of solar years.  This relationship shows 254 sidereal moon months for every 19 
solar years.  There are also 235 synodic months in the same period, which we know from 
the calculation above we are also capable of measuring using simply the full set of 
coordinates themselves.  
[A note about the Earth’s sidereal motion.  The 360 degree traversal is assumed to represent the sidereal 
rotation of the earth and thus represent one sidereal day, which it should be pointed out is 23 hours 56 
minutes, not 24 hours.  This small 4 minute discrepancy adds up to one day in a whole year due to the fact that 
one orbit of the sun is equivalent to a day itself.  To compensate for the solar day versus the sidereal day we 
only need to add another square for each 360 degree traversal.  This can be done by including the ? symbol 
from the lunar runoff as part of our row traversal.  The effect of this is that we move the equivalent of 24 hours 
rather than 23 hours 56 minutes, which is the Earth’s sidereal period.  Employing such a scheme however 
causes us to move one place back in the coordinate traversal for every 360 degree rotation.  For the initial 
computation shown in the computer model there is no adjustment for sidereal vs solar day.]

Part VI 
The Antikythera Mechanism  
For those not familiar with the Antikythera Mechanism it is essentially the remains of some 
heavily corroded metal gears found on a ship wreck off the island of Antikythera at the very 
beginning of the 20th century.  This strange artefact lay unclassified in a Greek museum for 
some time before archaeologists suddenly noted that the gearing ratios in the mechanism 
appeared to relate to celestial cycles.  As the sophistication of the device became apparent 
people started to refer to the mechanism as an object seemingly displaced in time as it 
encapsulated a detailed knowledge of celestial mechanics thought impossible to have been 
known at the time of construction.  In short the mechanism encapsulates the workings of 
both the Metonic and Saros cycles of 19 and approximately 18 years respectively.  These 
cycles are important as they can be used to predict the days on which solar and lunar 
eclipses are likely to occur.  The aspects of eclipse prediction will be revisited in a later 
section, but first lets acquaint ourselves with a reconstruction of what the Antikythera 
Mechanism looked like.  A good starting point as ever is the Wikipedia entry at: 
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism, but we should at least note here 
that there were three major dials that could be read off from the mechanism.  The 
mechanism had a front and back face, with the front having a single dial and the back having 
two dials.  There was a winding arm in the mechanism that would allow the operator to 
animate the device and show the evolution of the relative positions of the moon and 
planets with respect to time.  The largest remaining part of the mechanism recovered from 
the seabed can be seen below. It was only with the advent of advanced imaging techniques 
that the true complexity of the gearing became apparent.
