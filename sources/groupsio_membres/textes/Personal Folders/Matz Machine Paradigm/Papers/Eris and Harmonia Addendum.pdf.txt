Addendum to the original paper 
As an addition to this revised paper, new evidence is provided for the first time that shows a concrete 
rationale for the rail system previously put forward.  These observations also expose the order in 
which the 6 rails should be processed.  It should be noted that this new information on processing 
order and constraints negates the text provided for rails 3, 4  and 6 given in the original Eris & 
Harmonia paper.  Most importantly, this new evidence also gives a firm foundation to the idea that 
both the plain and cipher text must be viewed simultaneously in order to make sense of how K4 
should be processed. 
The US Dollar and K3 
 
Before examining the new evidence, we should first reacquaint ourselves with the design and detail of 
the US Dollar.  The Great Seal of the United States has on its reverse side a 13 stepped pyramid 
topped by a shining eye of providence along with the Latin phrase, ‘ANNUIT COEPTIS’, which is 
commonly taken to translate as ‘He has favoured our undertakings’.  If we examine the plain text and 
cipher text of Kryptos for K3 simultaneously we can make the following observation. 
 
In the plain text we have an anagram of the word AN(N)UIT that is deficient in one N and in the 
cipher text we have an anagram of COEPTI(S) that is deficient in one S.  The letters that take the 
place of the required S and N respectively are B and Y.  If we now superimpose the positions at which 
these two anagrams appear to make up a square in both the plain and cipher text then we note the 
emergence of new information that relates directly to the final sentence of the unaltered K2 plain text. 
 
The centre of the square produced in the plain text has at its centre 6 letters that are themselves an 
anagram of ID ROWS.  Given that the excess letters of the previous Annuit Coeptis anagram are BY, 
we end up with a concrete relationship to the final words in K2, which are ID BY ROWS.

The previous paper exposed the underlying rail system shown above correctly, but did not show any 
evidence for the order in which the rails themselves should be processed.  The solution to this 
problem was eventually resolved in August 2022 using software to examine letter frequencies in the 
rows where the rails occurred.  The resulting common string that appeared to be present in the regions 
of interest was found to be, YES ITS.   
YES ITS 
The ‘YES ITS’ result is of interest for two reasons.  The first was that it would appear to relate directly 
to the sentence in the diaries of Howard Carter that read, ‘yes, it is wonderful’, which proceeds the 
phrase, ‘can you seen anything?’.  The second reason is that it was also visible within the plain text as 
an anagram in a position that appeared to mirror the previous two plain text clues.

The Apex of Kryptos 
There is one final clue that remains to be discussed that is well in excess of the information provided 
within the scope of the previous paper.  If we complete the symmetrical scheme given to us by the 
previous 3 clues then we can logically derive a fourth, which is shown here for the anagram TOMB 
LR. 
 
Scanning for the letters TOMB in both the plaintext and the cipher text leads to the following result. 
 
There are two regions in the plaintext that give a contiguous correlation between all 4 letters and one 
in the cipher text.  If we take the clue from the 4th anagram literally then LR may be an indication that 
we should look for the word TOMB in the lower right, which is exactly where this second example is 
located.  
  

[[page 7]]
These two regions in the plaintext share a remarkable geometrical relationship in that their diagonal 
components can be extended upon and joined in order to form a triangle comprised of 13 rows that is 
capped by an I.   
 
We now appear to have come full circle in relation to the Great Seal of the United States as we started 
with the relationship ANNUIT COEPTIS and have now been guided towards the rendition of a 13 
stepped pyramid topped by an I.   
The centre line of the triangle in the cipher text rendition terminates on the D of USD in the first row.  
The plain text also has DUS in the vertical plane just to the right of this position, which is almost 
analogous to the original ANNUIT COEPTIS clue (shown in the next diagram).  The previous papers 
in this series have always taken USD as the logical starting point of the K4 decrypt, so it is somewhat 
remarkable to find these additional observations that would also point to the significance of the USD 
string.   
Another striking occurrence along the centre line in both the plain and cipher texts are the letters 
ANIT along the central axis of the triangle facing in different directions.  The author cannot help but 
wonder if this is in some way linked to the choice of name for Antipodes, which is the sister sculpture 
to Kryptos, currently on display at the Hirshhorn Museum in Washington DC.
