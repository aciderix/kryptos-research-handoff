Introduction and thanks 
This paper marks the culmination of some 11 years of research into Jim Sanborn’s Kryptos 
installation located on the grounds of the CIA Headquarters in Langley Virginia.  Since its original 
dedication in 1990 public awareness of the Kryptos cipher challenge has grown year on year.  The 
fact that only partial decryptions for three of the four ciphers have ever been publicly acknowledged 
have only served to increase its mystique amongst the amateur crypto community that has sprung up 
in its wake.  Its stubbornness to yield its final secrets even with the gradual release of some 25% of its 
plain text over the last decade does not appear to have changed the odds stacked against any would be 
cryptographer in any way.   
The framework and results presented herein will no doubt represent something of a paradigm shift in 
how both K4 and the sculpture itself as a ‘whole’ piece are currently viewed.  As will be seen from 
the eventual conclusion, the artist has not only given us four encrypted messages, but also the 
blueprints for two physical devices, the first of which would seemingly allow us to decode K4’s 
unique cipher scheme.  Seasoned programmers will know that for many years the computer journals 
ran regular columns that challenged readers to construct functional programs using the most minimal 
amount of code possible.  I would hope that after this paper has been digested, appraised and 
researched further that it will become apparent just how much has been squeezed into the 1735 letters 
that comprise Kryptos.  I would also hope that the technical brilliance of the installation as a complete 
scheme will finally be realised and that parts of this paper will eventually find their way into 
educational settings for replication in STEM based teaching activities.  On that basis please feel free 
to replicate any or all parts of this paper for use as an educational or research resource.  
November the 3rd 2021 will mark the 31st anniversary of the sculptures original dedication by the then 
Agency Director William Webster.  This in itself was also an important date as it came 31 years to the 
day after Dwight D Eisenhower laid the foundation stone for the Original Headquarters Building in 
1959.  At that same ceremony in 1959 a time capsule was buried that  supposedly ‘contained secrets’.  
Whether this was simply a joke made by Allan Dulles at the expense of the assembled press or not 
may never be known as the official manifest for the capsule did not appear to contain anything of any 
great secrecy, but what we do know is that 31 years later a sculpture was installed that really did 
contain secrets.  This paper won’t offer any background on the Kryptos story as it will be assumed 
that the reader is already familiar with the chronology of how the first three parts were broken.  It will 
concentrate instead purely on the nature of the proposed cipher method and the potential meaning of 
the final message. This solution ‘beyond the solution’ will put an entirely new perspective on the 
sculpture and show that it is in fact far more than a redundant copper sheet once K4 is broken and 
reveals instead a new function that takes it into perpetuity. 
This introduction would of course be incomplete without a sincere expression of gratitude to Jim 
Sanborn, Ed Scheidt, their patrons and any other unnamed parties behind the scenes for seeding the 
world with such a constructive and educational curiosity.  I hope this paper in itself goes some way to 
paying the necessary respect to their collective artistic and technical skills. 
Lastly I’d like to thank all those on the public forums, microsites and especially Larry McElhiney who 
moderates the Kryptos.groups.io site along with Matt Coleman over at the Kryptos Facebook Page 
and Elonka Dunin who took the time out to cast an eye over my initial paper back in 2010.  As 
someone that came late to this in 2009 I’d also like to thank Elonka as well as all the other crypto 
enthusiasts that continue to publicly communicate so much about the sculpture and cryptography in 
general to the outside world.  Anyone unfamiliar with Kryptos that wants additional background may 
also want to visit Elonka’s Kryptos site at https://elonka.com/kryptos/ before proceeding any further 
with this document.

Overview 
This paper presents evidence showing that the Kryptos cipher text is a palimpsest written over the top 
of 12 cryptograms, each of which show a consistent underlying structure that can be uncovered and 
decrypted using the K4 cipher text as a key.  A framework is put forward that shows the sculpture 
must first be closed to form a cylinder consisting of 32 columns and 28 rows.  Each row must then be 
given freedom to move about the axis in the same way as a Jefferson Cylinder.  Groupings of three 
consecutive rows are identified that give rise to  6 ‘rail positions’ on which all 12 solves are 
predicated.  Each of these rails are designated by the same 4 letter marker, with 3 being in the 
horizontal plane and 3 being in the vertical.  Two distinct types of symmetry operation are used to 
mirror any row shifts in the relation n* = 29 – n , where n is the row number being shifted.  These two 
algorithms are provided via cryptically hidden crib examples which are then used repeatedly for the 
remaining solves.  A defined start and end position is found for each rail position based on the 
principle of ‘invariance under transformation’, which naturally gives rise to the identification of 8 
letters for each rail configuration where there are two configurations per rail.  The positioning of the 
rails that are marked in the vertical plane along with the information that can be gleaned from the 
BERLIN CLOCK plain text, shows that a transition occurs at Rail Position 3, from which point on the 
letters are read off vertically rather than horizontally.  In each case the algorithms naturally give rise 
to the identification of 8 letters in each cryptogram.  Once each cryptogram has been broken, all 
cipher and plain text is right shifted by one letter in the Kryptos alphabet.  In order to read the plain 
text for the 8 letters in each cryptogram it is necessary to first encrypt them using the same Vigenere 
scheme used for parts K1 and K2, using the K4 cipher text as the key.           
Amendments to the previous paper 
At the time of publishing this is believed to be an original piece of work that draws upon and extends 
the findings already put into the public domain by the author’s previous paper, ‘Proposed Outline 
Method For Decryption of K4’, published to the Kryptos Group forum in May 2020.  This date was of 
course prior to the latest ‘EAST’ clue gradually emerging via group member Sukhwant Singh’s posts 
that occurred later in August of that year.  The new clue highlighted a discrepancy in the previous 
work whereby the  retrieval of ‘NORTH EAST’ in the correct positions was achievable in isolation, 
but could not be used to successfully recover ‘EAST NORTH EAST’.   
I’m indebted to James Churchill a member of the Kryptos Group’s sister site on Facebook for an 
observation around repeating letter strings that he shared on December 15th 2020.  This intriguing 
pattern in the K2 cipher text represented the break that was needed in order to explain why the new 
‘EAST’ clue could not be accommodated within the original framework despite the general premise 
being sound.  Most importantly, it also highlighted a viable basis for how the previous framework 
could be amended in order to achieve a full solve, the results of which are now discussed in detail.  
Results 
The modified method put forward in this paper generates a coherent plain text for the remaining K4 
cipher as well as all verified plain text letters for K4 in their correct positions.  As ever, this is a large 
document and the author makes no apologies for that as a full set of cipher and plain text that has been 
correctly transformed by the appropriate right shift in the Kryptos alphabet has been provided should 
anyone wish to test or refine any of the results for themselves.  This paper may represent something of 
an initial pass at the final plain text, but the general basis of the method is generated from first 
principles and is believed to be sound.  The complete K4 string derived in this paper is 96 letters in 
length with a 97th letter (R) that remains unmapped, giving an overall string of.

The forward slashes represent my interpretation of how the message should be split, but is informed

are discussed as part of the conclusion.  This would appear at first glance to be a reasonably cryptic 
message replete with as many spelling mistakes as K1-3 put together, but there are aspects of it that 
give reason for confidence that portions of it at the very least (beyond the known plain text) are 
indeed correct.  The attributes of this message that are deemed to be of interest are discussed in detail 
later, but before we procced to the method it is perhaps worth taking the 1000 foot view and looking 
at two subtle features that follow from the nature of the general method used. 
The first is that the proposed solution is predicated on the existence of 6 ‘Rail Positions’.  Each of 
these Rail Positions consist of 3 consecutive rows of 32 letters, which sum in total to 96 letters.  As 
we have a single letter remaining in K4 once we are done it can readily be seen that the plain text 
eventually creates a result that fits perfectly into the exact same dimensions as one of these standard 
Rail Positions. 
The second and perhaps most subtle feature of this method is what at first glance may appear to be 
just a numerical coincidence, but equally may also be the intentional signature of the master puzzle 
makers that have put this entire scheme together. 
It will be seen shortly in the discussion on method that we are selecting 8 characters for each 
cryptogram over rows that are 32 letters in length.  Those that have researched the CIA’s Langley 
campus for any additional art, inscriptions or seals looking for potential clues will know that etched 
into the wall of the original building's main lobby is a biblical verse which also characterizes the 
intelligence mission in a free society. It reads:

Prerequisites 
In order to successfully replicate the results described in this paper, a software tool that is capable of 
applying the following transforms to the Kryptos cipher and plain Text is recommended, but this is 
purely for expedience.  The results presented could equally be derived in long hand fashion simply 
using ‘pencil and paper’, the Kryptos cipher text and its associated tableau.  The recommended 
transform capabilities for any software tool are: 
• 
A method of showing the plaintext and cipher text side by side that allows transforms to be 
applied to both sides simultaneously.  This leads to a display with four panels visible on 
screen as shown below. 
• 
The shifting of individual rows to the right or left over a field of 32 characters, whereby a 
right shift will make the current 32nd character the 1st character and shift all subsequent 
character positions right by 1 position. 
• 
A method of reciprocating any transform (or its opposite) in row n with a corresponding 
transform in row (29-n).  This is a symmetrical transform that will be explained later. 
• 
A method of transforming the cipher and plain text in bulk by right shifting through the 
KRYPTOS alphabet by one letter at a time.  (This operation can be done by hand by simply 
utilising a single row from the decryption tableau as a guide). 
Screenshots used in this paper make use of bespoke software written by the author in order to achieve 
the functional requirements outlined above, but written tables are provided at the end of the paper in 
order to allow access to the same outputs as the software. 
As can be seen here, the software tool gives a simultaneous view of both the plaintext and the cipher 
text.  Any positional shifts applied to a given row are made in both views.  Row 23 extends over 33 
characters, but this is compensated for by the software, despite not showing in the matrix view below.

Creating a physical rendition of the cipher machine 
 
Before discussing the decryption method in detail, we need to first construct a physical device that we 
will use for all steps of the K4 decryption process.  The design of this device is not arbitrary, but is 
instead an informed design choice based on Jim Sanborn’s Antipodes sculpture that was created in 
1992 and is now on display at the Hirshhorn Museum in Washington DC.   
 
 
 
 
The design of Antipodes design as seen above would suggest that if we wrap the copper sheets like a 
scroll then it is possible to create two cylinders, one within the other.  If we remove the extraneous 
letters T and L from both the cipher and tableau panels respectively then we can see that by putting 
the text into a regular matrix arrangement the cipher text has dimensions of 32 columns by 28 rows 
and similarly the tableau text has dimensions of 31 columns by 28 rows.   
 

[[page 9]]
9 
 
With these dimensions in mind we can see that it is possible to close both the cipher text and tableau 
panels into two closed cylinders, one of which will fit neatly within the other. 
 
To complete the necessary construction of our cipher cylinder we need to add some additional kinetic 
elements.  Firstly we will need to allow the inner cylinder to rotate freely about the axis.  This means 
that the inner tableau cylinder will always be matched against 31 letters of the outer 32 letter cipher 
cylinder meaning that rotation about the central axis will cause different letters from the inner cylinder 
to be juxtaposed against different  letters of the outer cipher text cylinder each time it moves by 1 
letter position.  The functionality that this feature provides will be discussed shortly. 
The outer cipher text cylinder needs to have its 28 individual rows of 32 letters broken apart and given 
the freedom to move freely about the central axis in that same way as a Jefferson Cylinder.  I’ve taken 
this rendition of a Jefferson Cylinder from the web and reworked it slightly, so it is deficient in 4 of 
the 28 rows, but shows the general concept of how we will need the rows to be able to move. 

[[page 10]]
10 
 
 
 
The final feature that we will need to add is reasonably unique and I would keen to hear from any 
readers of this paper as to whether they can propose a more elegant solution to this design requirement 
than the one I outline below. 
As there are 28 individual rows there will naturally be an axis of symmetry between rows 14 and 15. 
Our final design requirement is that for any rotation of a particular row we will require the ability to 
shift the corresponding reciprocal row (about the axis of symmetry) either symmetrically or counter-
symmetrically about the central axis.  We will revert shortly to an idealised theoretical version of this 
setup whereby we only require one cylinder, but from a mechanical perspective its possible to achieve 
this functionality by creating a second replica of the cipher text cylinder and inverting it so that row 1 
is at the bottom rather the top.  Attaching a belt between rows of the two cylinders then gives 
symmetrical movement about the axis for the reciprocal rows.  Attaching the belt in a twisted or 
figure of eight configuration would allow for counter symmetrical rotation instead.

which make use of the plaintext, so our final step will be to create a second version of this device that

cylinder, we shall simply fill in with the standard K4 cipher text.  Intriguingly, the idea of two 
cylinders existing one as a counterpart to the other has already been alluded in other works by Jim 
Sanborn such as the 2001 ‘Lux’ installation in Fort Meyers.

Rail Positions 
After some exhaustive analysis to determine their uniqueness,  it became apparent that the cipher text 
cylinder has six defined ‘rail position’ markers that are each defined by a distinct pattern of the 
repeating letters, R, E, V and F.   
A ROT13 operation on these same letters gives ERIS.  As Eris is also the name of the Greek goddess 
of chaos, strife and discord it was thought fitting that she should be entrusted as the guardian of K4’s 
concealment.  Her counterpart in mythology is Harmonia, who symbolises union and marriage.  The 
solution described herein seeks to gradually bring together a number of seemingly disparate clues 
into a single unified solution hence the title of this paper.  .

Rail Positions 5 & 6 
Rail positions 5 and 6 are both slightly anomalous for two very different, but related reasons.  The 
overall solution will eventually be seen to be dependent on the use of two types of symmetry 
operation that occur in conjunction with the reciprocal rows for each of these rails.  With regards Rail 
Position 5 this is impossible as the rows do not sit evenly across the axis of symmetry, which may 
account for why Jim Sanborn felt compelled to give BERLIN as the first clue as this would flush out 
any would be solves that may have stalled at this particular step.  The method used on Rail Position 5 
still uses the same general principles as the rest of the puzzle, but dispenses with the symmetry 
operations used by the other rails.

mirrors the positions for Rail Position 4 that have themselves already used the underlying symmetry 
relationships for those rows.  If the puzzle maker did not take action to address this issue then the 
general solutions to Rail Position 4 could also be used to instantly break the alignment settings for 
Rail Position 6.

Conventions used and glossary of terms 
It would appear that we are dealing with a unique cipher type and as a result we will have to define 
some conventions and also introduce some new terms in order to best describe the decryption process 
concisely.  The drafting of these terms will be seen to pay dividends later by allowing us to reduce the 
K4 decryption to a simple two-step process that is repeated for all 12 cryptograms.  
C(n).  The cryptogram number.  This paper puts forward the idea that K4 is made up of 12 discrete 
cryptograms, which shall be labelled C(1) to C12). 
KRS(n) In the discussion on the nature of the physical cipher device it was noted that the inner 
cylinder on which the tableau is written can rotate by one letter position at a time.  In order to emulate 
the effect of the changing juxtaposition of tableau letters against cipher text or plain text letters we 
shall simulate this by right shifting by one letter value each time using the Kryptos alphabet. 
KRYPTOSABCDEFGHIJLMNQUVWXZ 
To perform a right shift operation on any arbitrary piece of text using the Kryptos alphabet we simply 
need to look up the current position for any letter of that text in the alphabet above and move one 
position to the right.  The most simple example to use would of course be the word KRYPTOS itself.  
It can be seen that a right shift of one letter through the Kryptos alphabet will result in the letters 
RYPTOSA being returned.  Another subsequent right shift operation would result in the letters 
YPTOSAB being our next output.  Every time we break a cryptogram for a given rail configuration a 
global ‘Kryptos Right Shift’ is applied to all of the plain and cipher text on both of our cylinders.  
There is no KRS applied for C(1), so its initial setting is n=0, but all subsequent right shifts 
performed are KRS(n+1) every time we have extracted a cryptogram and are about to move to either 
the next configuration or the next rail position. 
RP(n).  The rail position number 1-6.  Each rail position will have two configurations. 
SA(CS/SS).  The two configurations for each rail position correspond to the algorithm that is 
necessary to apply in each instance.  Odd numbered cryptograms will always have the counter-
Symmetric algorithm applied (CS) and even numbered cryptograms will always make use of the 
simple-symmetric algorithm (SS). 
For each of the two rail position configurations we shall define three alignment settings, which are the 
IRP, NSP and TSP.   
IRP is the ‘initial row position’ and is useful to document each time, so that anyone wishing to verify 
these results or do more research can simply cut and paste the text, which will already have had the 
correct amount of right shifting applied.   

[[page 17]]
17 
 
NSP is the ‘normalised symmetry position’ and is the row alignment in which 3 letter mirroring 
within the row can be seen as well as the characters that will remain ‘invariant under transformation’ 
when we move the middle rail to the ‘transformed symmetry position’.  
TSP is the ‘transformed symmetry position’ in which the middle row of the rail will have its invariant 
letters shifted to their second possible configuration other than the NSP. 
IUT 2:2[i/j] These are the 4 letters that remain ‘invariant under transformation’ from the NSP to the 
TSP.  Letters in the top or bottom row will be defined as i and the letters in the middle row as j. 
IUT 3:4[i/j] These are the 3 letters that initially occur in a contiguous block that is either horizontal, 
split or vertical in the NSP that will remain invariant and be present in the TSP position as 3 out of 4 
letters of a 2:2 block of 4 letters. 
IUT H:V[i/j] The i/j parameters are for letters that will be read in the horizontal plane for the NSP 
and in the vertical plane for the TSP. 
RML[ijk].  These are the 3 letters that are mirrored within the row for any given NSP. 
SML[ijk].  These are the letters that are mirrored between the rail position rows and their reciprocal 
row positions when the counter-symmetrical algorithm has been run to arrive at the TSP. 
It is appreciated that these terms and conventions may initially seem complex before viewing the plain 
text derivations, but it will allow for all cryptograms to be broken on a repeatable basis using a 
common template that will make for easy comparison between cases. 
R(n) For any given rail position we shall use this to denote a specific rail, namely 1, 2 or 3. 
RS/LS(n) The amount of left or right shift that has been applied to a given rail between 
transformations.  For all of the 12 cryptograms it is only ever rail 2 that is shifted left or right in order 
to transition from the NSP to the TSP. 
LBA. We shall refer to a ‘letter block alignment’ as two letters that sit directly over each other 
between rows. 
A brief note on Vigenère encryption 
The final part of the decryption process appears to cause some confusion even amongst those familiar 
with the cryptographic techniques involved.  The principle of symmetrical encryption means that just 
like the rest of the framework that has utilised symmetry to some degree, the final method of decoding 
is no different.  When using the Vigenère scheme, the use of encryption is purely the reversal of the 
process that was used to decrypt parts one and two.  The puzzle makers have chosen to only reveal the 
final plain text when the mirror image of the original decryption process is undertaken.  An example 
is given here for OBKRUOX, the first 7 letters of K4. 
The key required to decrypt OBKRUOX to give a plain text KRYPTOS is OCYTZDT. 
The plain text required to be encrypted with a key of OBKRUOX to give a cipher text of KRYPTOS 
is UNYYCKB. 
The puzzle makers have opted to use this second method instead.  As the process is symmetrical the 
level of cryptographic security is identical.  This means that the end result that is read as plain text is 
‘technically’ classified as cipher text, but the output of this process for each 8 letter cryptogram is 
clearly intended to be read as the plain text.

General Method 
The discovery of the general method and how it was derived from the two crib examples that 
accompany cryptograms 1 and 2 is discussed in a low level of detail in part 3, but for those not 
looking to replicate results and that purely want to see the end results then part 2 will give something 
of a pictorial overview of the complete scheme.  The underlying principle is reasonably simple, but is 
made obscure by the application of two additional layers of obfuscation.   
The basis on which the entire scheme is predicated is that of ‘invariance under transformation’ or 
IUT.

type of IUT only starts to appear from C(7) onwards.

Degrees of freedom 
The scientific method would typically urge caution at this point as we should always be vigilant for 
false positives given the number of numerical coincidences that abound in this universe.  The question 
we should always ask is, ‘no matter how good these results are, what are the statistical chances of 
them being coincidental’.  An answer to this question was sought through programmatic means and 
the answer was much higher than might be expected for comfort.  The occurrence of symmetrical 
letter occurrences in matching groups of three within each rail position are actually much higher than 
intuition would suggest. 
The puzzle maker would appear to have reduced at least some of the available degrees of freedom by 
the selection of configurations that give rise to consistent phenomena in the NSP and TSP positions, 
but there is still a sense that the smoking gun is still at large in terms of being sure that each NSP and 
TSP position is unique.  What is needed is a programmatic way to select these alignments in such a 
way where we can be certain that no other possibilities exist.  It may well be that we need to make an 
additional observation to close that gap or conduct a re-appraisal of the data already presented.  
Irrespective of these insecurities about the uniqueness of the NSP and TSP positions, we shall now 
continue to C(7) and beyond.

A solution beyond the solution 
The reference to something astronomical in nature combined with the mention of a chronometer that 
is related to a clock or clocks in Berlin was a satisfying message to extract as the author’s two 
previous ‘Machine’ papers published in 2017 have addressed these very issues of celestial mechanics 
and how they would appear to relate to the overall sculpture layout. For anyone that hasn’t read those 
papers, they assert that all 4 panels of the sculpture participate in a schematic that can be overlayed on 
top of the sculpture text as a hidden steganographic layer.   
These two previous papers covered in some detail how this hidden layer can be used to calculate the 
Metonic and Saros cycles, which are also the two main functions provided by the Anitkythera 
Mechanism, which is acknowledged as being one of the worlds earliest analogue computers.

Brilliant Chronometers 
Much has been made of the various clocks of interest that reside in Berlin, but there are perhaps two 
that are of real interest for the purposes of this conclusion.  The first of course is Dieter Binninger’s 
‘Mengenlehreuhr’ or set theory clock and the second is Bernard Gitton’s ‘Clock of Flowing Time’ 
that extends over 3 floors of the Europa Center in Berlin. 
The steganographic layout that we find within Kryptos would appear to be a hybrid of these two 
concepts, both of which are driven by the presence of an ingenious gearing system hidden within the 
actual coordinates themselves.  The presence of this gear mechanism is not arbitrary and is instead 
found in a clue that can be extracted when looking at the plain text through the filter of the 
steganographic overlay.  If we examine the boundaries of the 19/13 row scheme when viewing rows

38 + 57 + 77 + 8 = 180 
This hour to minute relationship forms the backbone of a gearing system that we can drive by treating 
the sculpture as an analogue computer driven by cursor operations, whereby each letter can light up 
when selected as part of some predefined function.  In the physical case of something like one of 
Bernard Gitton’s water clocks this could also be thought of as filling a container with a set amount of 
water before filling up the next.  At some logical point the sum (in this case 180) is reached and we 
would then drain the water away to start the process again of filling again. 
It should be noted that the coordinate system used in the Kryptos stego overlay is schematic and not 
physical, by which it is meant that after counting out 38 blocks we then need to return to the start of 
the three initial rows in order to count out 57.  Whilst this is not an issue when programming such a 
scheme it would be an issue if making a physical reproduction as another 38 blocks would be needed.  
Anyone reading the authors previous papers looking to make physical interpretations of the scheme 
should bear this in mind when sketching out their initial designs.

look no further than the computation of the K1 and K2 decrypts, which can both be performed by the 
sculpture by driving it as an analogue computation using these same cursor operations.  If we in fact 
leave the letters that have participated in that computational process highlighted after decrypting K1

If we use the highlighted blocks for the westerly and northerly seconds as positions to mark our days 
then we can now chart the synodic cycle for Venus using the following steps. 
Venus appears as a morning star for 263 days, which we can track in the light blue area in the top 
panel. 
Venus then disappears for 50 days behind the Sun, which can be tracked in the gold block given by 
the 44 plus 6. 
Venus is then an evening star for another 263 days which we can mark in the light blue area in the 
bottom panel. 
Finally, Venus transits in front of the Sun and is not visible for 8 days, which we can record in the 8 
minute block of our coordinate scheme highlighted above in orange. 
The cycle then repeats.  As we can simultaneously set off a cursor in the calendar portion of the 
panels it will of course be possible to determine the dates on which superior and inferior conjunctions 
will occur.  As we saw with the Metonic and Saros cycles, the Synodic Cycle of Venus also gives rise 
to regularly defined intervals of time, but in this instance there is something of an added bonus as the 
position in the sky at which these conjunctions occur give a well-defined and recurring geometric

Further Reading 
For anyone wanting to read more about the various subjects that have been touched upon in this paper 
I would recommend the following books. 
‘A Portable Cosmos’ by Alexander Jones is highly recommended.  This is an excellent work on both 
the history and reconstructed mechanics of the Antikythera Mechanism.   
ISBN 978-0-19-973934-9 
I would also recommend Carl Sagan’s ‘Contact’ novel to anyone that has an active interest in 
Kryptos.  Whether Carl Sagan was a consultee to the Kryptos project or not will no doubt be known in 
time, but I can’t recommend this book highly enough.  For anyone that has seen the film, but not read 
the book, the endings are different with one being more profound than the other.   
ISBN 0-09-946950-2 
Simon Singh’s ‘The Code Book’ is still a classic introduction to Cryptography for the modern reader 
and I recently had chills upon noticing that the books acknowledgements include thanks for the 
brilliant Sonya Holbraad who was once also my maths tutor at a pivotal point in my academic career.     
ISBN 185-70-2889-9 
Codebreaking A Practical Guide, by Elonka Dunin and Klaus Schmeh is probably one of the most 
recent introductory cryptography books to hit the shelves and has a great level of depth for anyone 
wanting to take their interest further. 
ISBN 978-1472144218 
Cubed: The Puzzle of Us All is another new comer to the book shelves and is written by Erno Rubik 
the inventor of the cube that bears his name.  This short, but fun book is not just about puzzles, but 
more importantly about the role of the creative process, so educators may also want to pick up a copy 
of this one and surreptitiously leave it lying in view of any students with curious minds.   
ISBN 978-1474613118 
Other than Rubik’s work on ‘invariance under transformation’ there appears to be no other record of a 
similar puzzle type cipher that I can find a reference to other than an academic paper by Douglas W 
Mitchell from 1992 entitled “Rubik's Cube” as a transposition device.  This paper doesn’t appear to 
point back to anything relating to the method discussed in this paper, but I would be interested to hear 
from anyone that can find precedence for this scheme.

Afterward 
In compressing these findings into a paper that is almost academic in nature it is appreciated that a 
large part of any potential audience may have been lost, along also with any sense of the narrative and 
the sense of adventure that ran through each of these discoveries.  Please bear in mind that each one 
represented an individual moment of wonder, intrigue or elation rather than the clinical end results 
discussed in the previous sections.  This is a long paper and there will no doubt be the need for many 
corrections and clarifications as it has been written in isolation, but it is hoped that professionals and 
amateurs alike in the crypto community will kick the tyres on both the IUT scheme as well as the 
assertion that Kryptos when viewed as a holistic piece is also an analogue calculator capable of a wide
