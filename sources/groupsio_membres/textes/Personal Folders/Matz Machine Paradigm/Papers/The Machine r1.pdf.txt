Introduction and Thanks 
 
The following paper is essentially a more detailed retelling of the previous paper entitled ‘Valkyrie’ 
that has been languishing in the file uploads area of the Yahoo Kryptos Group since October 2013.  
The presence of the paper in the file uploads wasn’t widely telegraphed as it was a place holder, 
summarizing discoveries to that date that fell well short of any meaningful conclusions.  That paper 
also omitted any mention of the holistic nature of the sculpture itself.  In the intervening time we 
have had another clue revealed by Jim Sanborn, which is the word CLOCK for positions 70 through to 
74. 
As usual the readership is aimed primarily at those in the Kryptos K4 community who are already 
conversant with the cipher and plain texts of sections K1-K3 as well as the associated tools and 
techniques for the relevant decryptions.  This is a lengthy paper, but no apologies are given for that 
other than for it being perhaps too brief in places.  I have endeavoured to include enough detail to 
make the reconstruction of the results as clear as possible.  Since the initial draft I’ve edited quite 
brutally in places in order to add focus and additional clarity to the core concept.  There will no doubt 
be some mistakes and some areas where I could have expanded more with clearer examples and 
diagrams, but the general premise presented herein is sound and self-consistent, so please feel free 
to copy any material in this paper in order to illustrate any corrections as part of your own work.  I’m 
happy for this work to be redistributed in its entirety or drawn upon in parts, but please include a 
citation.  Should you wish to make a physical reconstructions of the machine outlined in this paper 
then remember that all the intellectual property rights for the design remain with Jim Sanborn. 
As with sections K1, K2 and K3 anyone wishing to repeat these results for themselves needs only a 
basic knowledge of cryptography, a pen and some paper.  
This introduction would be incomplete without a sincere expression of gratitude to Jim Sanborn, Ed

Abstract 
 
A number of what are thought to be novel discoveries are hereby put into the public domain for wider 
discussion and verification.  The core finding is that the Kryptos cipher text is layered on top of a 
numerical surface that is independent of the lettering itself.  This surface represents a hardware 
model on which to perform an analogue computation of the moons cycles in relation to the solar day 
and year.  As a result of this it is conjectured that the sculpture is in fact a symbolic representation of 
the Antikythera mechanism.  It will be shown that the sculpture is capable of recording time at the 
level of seconds, minutes, hours, days and weeks.  It can further be used to capture the calendar, 
sidereal and synodic months.  The largest time span it records is the 76 year Callippic cycle, which is 
composed of 4 Metonic cycles each of 19 years in length.  It is also shown that the two decryption 
tableau panels can be used to record subsequent Saros cycles and accurately keep track of the 
exeligmos between eclipses once their initial location and date is identified.  In presenting this 
hypothesis there is a reappraisal of the context in which K3 should be decrypted and indications are 
given as to which avenues might be explored further to fully decrypt K4 relating to data manipulation 
and the recovery of the correct algorithmic process for letter selection.  Though there are many new 
insights in this paper the most important is that of putting a new perspective on the sculpture in that 
it is far more than a redundant copper scroll once all the eventual plaintext is known and reveals 
instead a new function that takes it into perpetuity.

Part I 
Background 
 
The following information on the background of the Kryptos sculpture was gathered from a range of 
internet sources. 
Kryptos was dedicated on November the 3rd 1990 on the grounds of the CIA headquarters in Langley 
Virginia.  The main body of the sculpture is a worked copper scroll by the Artist Jim Sanborn who was 
commissioned by the CIA to create a series of installation pieces for the buildings courtyard.  He was 
additionally loaned the services of a retiring CIA cryptographic expert by the name of Ed Scheidt who 
was on hand to provide assistance with the cryptographic elements of the underlying text.  
Since 1990 there have been three public acknowledgments in relation to the decryption of parts K1, 
K2 and K3. 
A civilian computer scientist named Jim Gillogly went public with a decryption of parts K1, K2 and K3 in 
1999 having used computational analysis to arrive at the plain texts. 
After this announcement it was revealed that a CIA employee named David Stein was recognised 
within the CIA as having cracked parts K1, K2 and K3 using a pencil and paper technique in 1998.  
It was later announced in 2005 that an NSA team led by Ken Miller, Dennis McDaniels and two other 
unnamed individuals had deciphered parts K1, K2 & K3 in late 1992 using computational analysis. 
In the years since Gillogly’s announcement a mystique has continued to grow around the unsolved K4 
cipher text.  This is partly due to its resistance to yield its final secrets, but also the mass of interest 
aroused worldwide by Dan Brown’s code sleuthing novels and the growing pervasiveness of 
cryptography in modern life.  As a result there has been a mass of internet based collaboration in the 
public domain ever since, whereby common interest groups and individuals have persistently poured 
over the clues left in the plain texts of parts K1, K2 and K3 in search of possible breaks. 
For anyone not familiar with the layout of the sculptures original cipher text or the plain texts of K1, 
K2 and K3 then they read as follows.

Interventions post decryption of K1-K3 
 
Issues with the decryption of K2 
 
With regards the correct decryption of K2, Jim Sanborn the artist that created Kryptos 
intervened personally on April 19th 2006, contacting the group moderators in order to say, 
"No, that last part is wrong."  Elonka’s Kryptos website at www.elonka.com/kryptos relays 
the following about this incident. 
“He also indicated that there was a missing character on the sculpture, probably something 
that would have resulted in a plaintext "X" before that section. He said that he had thought 
that with the missing character, the section in question would have come out to be an 
unintelligible scramble. Instead, he was astounded to see that by sheer chance, the resulting 
random text had turned out to be apparently intelligible English, "ID BY ROWS", although 
that was not what was intended.” 
The Berlin Clue 
 
On the 21st November 2010 Jim Sanborn once again intervened, but in this instance it was to 
reveal a clue.  Sanborn indicated that a six letter string between positions 64 through to 69 
of the Kryptos plaintext for K4 would read BERLIN once decrypted. 
The Clock Clue 
 
On the 20th November 2014, Jim Sanborn revealed another clue in the form of the word 
CLOCK being the plaintext for positions 70 through to 74.  The fact that 11 letters of the 
plaintext were now known now massively reduced the odds of being able to find suitable 
decryption schemes.

Part II  
 
It will become evident throughout each step that the whole of the Kryptos sculpture 
represents a larger body of work than previously realised and is only eventually seen for 
what it is by solving multiple smaller puzzles.  Some of these steps are more significant than 
others, but in total they add up to a single conclusion that is both self-consistent and 
intellectually satisfying.  Viewed as a whole it will be seen that the piece seemingly steps us 
through the history of cryptography itself as a result of the smaller puzzles that we are 
expected to solve.  This is done quite brilliantly and bears testament to the intellect and 
artistry of both Scheidt, Sanborn and perhaps others.  I suspect that a further study of the 
cipher and plain texts after this paper has been distributed will yield many more encryption 
methods, some of which may light a way to a decryption path that does not require 
foreknowledge of the BERLIN and CLOCK clues respectively.

Examination of the plaintext for hints 
 
Like all encryption schemes, K1, K2 and K3 have weaknesses that made them vulnerable to 
cryptographic analysis and attack.  K4 has so far resisted any form of conventional 
cryptographic attack, which implies the sculpture text for K4 taken at face value is either not 
a computable quantity or if it is then uses an encryption scheme that leaves no statistical 
artefacts in its wake that can be exploited by the cryptographer in order to reconstruct the 
underlying scheme.   
The alternative is that we have to manipulate the sculpture text in some way in order to 
make it a computable surface and that K4 somehow interacts with this, rather than being a 
distinct cipher in its own right.   
The necessary manipulations that we need to make in order to make the sculpture 
computable are given by a series of clues present in the plain text of K1, K2 and K3. 
There are multiple ‘lucky breaks’ that can be made by analysing different clues in the 
plaintext, but significantly any of these initial entry points lead to the same eventual 
conclusion.

Part III 
Steganography 
Before embarking on this next section we need to clarify an important point.  The grid 
system that the Kryptos letters have been put into is an abstraction, just as geometry is an 
abstraction of the real world.  The individual blocks making the grid have a number value 
just like coordinates on an X and Y axis.  To give an example, three grid blocks means the 
number three irrespective of the letters that may be in each box.  I make this point as we 
have already made a manipulation of the text, but this has no bearing on the steganography 
that has been used in Kryptos, so for clarity I shall show the number surface using the 
original layout rather than with the re-arrangement of the first row used in the previous 
section.   
This abstraction is a powerful one as we shall see that the numerical values associated with 
the grid system can be thought of as an analogue of the modern day hardware model.  The 
letters themselves can be seen as being data that fill the registers of this hardware and our 
decryption technique can be thought of as an algorithm that is run using the underlying 
mechanics of this machine.  This may sound confusing at first given that Kryptos is purely a

Part V 
The Antikythera Mechanism  
For those not familiar with the Antikythera Mechanism it is essentially the remains of some 
heavily corroded metal gears found on a ship wreck off the island of Antikythera at the very 
beginning of the 20th century.  This strange artefact lay unclassified in a Greek museum for 
some time before archaeologists suddenly noted that the gearing ratios in the mechanism 
appeared to relate to celestial cycles.  As the sophistication of the device became apparent 
people started to refer to the mechanism as an object seemingly displaced in time as it 
encapsulated a detailed knowledge of celestial mechanics thought impossible to have been 
known at the time of construction.  In short the mechanism encapsulates the workings of 
both the Metonic and Saros cycles of 19 and approximately 18 years respectively.  These 
cycles are important as they can be used to predict the days on which solar and lunar 
eclipses are likely to occur.  The aspects of eclipse prediction will be revisited in a later 
section, but first lets acquaint ourselves with a reconstruction of what the Antikythera 
Mechanism looked like.  A good starting point as ever is the Wikipedia entry at: 
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism, but we should at least note here 
that there were three major dials that could be read off from the mechanism.  The 
mechanism had a front and back face, with the front having a single dial and the back having 
two dials.  There was a winding arm in the mechanism that would allow the operator to 
animate the device and show the evolution of the relative positions of the moon and 
planets with respect to time.  The largest remaining part of the mechanism recovered from 
the seabed can be seen below. It was only with the advent of advanced imaging techniques 
that the true complexity of the gearing became apparent.

Putting it all together 
We should not be at all surprised that we are being asked to theoretically reconstruct some 
kind of machine in order to gain deeper insights into how we might finally break Kryptos.  
There is an important precedent already set in the history of cryptography in that both the

Part IX 
Conclusions 
We’ve seen how the machine once reconstructed works as a timepiece capable of recording 
time, days, months and years over a 76 year period.  We can also see that the panels making 
up the decryption tableau can be used to record subsequent Saros cycles and by extension 
calculate the date of the exeligmos with reasonable accuracy.  Whether or not there is an 
analogue corresponding to the third face of the Antikythera mechanism remains to be seen, 
but the inclusion of the anagrams of HYDRA (a constellation in the plane of the ecliptic), SOL 
and DIANA (the Roman name for the moon goddess) in K3 implies that there may well be an 
as yet undiscovered analogue to the front face of the Antikythera mechanism that has not 
been covered in this paper.    
Something that hasn’t escaped my attention and that I’ve spent many walks pondering is 
another aspect of ‘The Machine’ that Kryptos forces us to contend with.  In 1990 Quantum 
Cryptography would have been at the very cutting edge of computational and mathematical 
research, whilst today we take it for granted as a reality of our ever increasing range of 
technological capabilities.  If we think about the way that we have been stepped through 
the history of cryptography by exposure to so many of the techniques of the past is it not 
possible that the very concept of Kryptos is a nod towards its future?  To be more specific 
Quantum Mechanics deals with the difficult philosophical questions that arise around the 
definition of reality and its relationship to measurement.  The sculpture itself is inert, but as 
we’ve seen our observations of its mathematical properties breathe an imitation of our 
Solar System into its very being.  When we stop looking then it returns to being an inert 
copper scroll latent with any possible meaning that anyone wishes to ascribe to it.  To break 
Kryptos is to somehow extract information from it and pass information back in to the point 
where the observed and observer are reflected in some kind of symbiotic computation.  
Having read around the subject matter of Quantum Mechanics in the past I’m not sure I can 
find any better analogue of its mysteries than the one presented to us by Kryptos itself.
