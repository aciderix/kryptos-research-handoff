 
 
 
 Monet's Kryptos Observations : the viability of acquiring the K1 and K2 keywords, PALIMPSEST and ABSCISSA, by cribbing the Morse code messages 
 
 

 
   Barking 
  up the wrong Tree   
  There appears to 
  be a common acceptance of a theory that the  K1  or  K2  
  keywords,   PALIMPSEST   
  and   ABSCISSA  , 
  could have been acquired by cribbing the Morse code messages against the K1 
  CT or K2 CT. 
  Or that this method was one that Sanborn and Scheidt had expected their op to 
  use.  
  The following displays 
  ought to put a damper on any such acceptance. 
  Sure, some of the cribbing reveals parts of the keywords, but unless one had 
  a computer, 
  the amount of work required - to do even a cursory search for viable fragments 
  - would be an immense undertaking.  
  Given Keyword:   
  DIGETALINTERPRETATUTISYOURPOSITIONVIRTUALLYINVISIBLESHADOWFORCESLUCIDMEMORYRQSOS 
   (Morse code messages, 80 characters long)   
  The order is the 
  same as that on the outcroppings, from South to North.  
  Every letter except 
  JKXZ, which don't occur in the Morse, will reveal part of the keyword for the 
  segment, PALIMPSEST for K1, ABSCISSA for K2.  
  However, the main letter grouping 
  culprits are:  
  INVI, POS, SIBLE, OUR, ESL, 
  FOR, INT, IRT, SHAD, TION, ION, ALLY, IN and LI.  
  Additionally, because 
  of the given order:   
  NVI (POSITIO NVI RTUALLU)  and  
  LESHAD (INVISIB LESHAD OW).
