- decrypt of Kryptos Part 3
(derived from Carter, Howard, The Tomb of Tutankhamen (1923))
For fifteen years, KRYPTOS has stood guard to a secret.  Contained in the last 97 characters of its encrypted text is
a some information, perhaps a vital clue to the whereabouts of the mysterious item buried somewhere on the
grounds of the CIA as described in a previously decrypted section.  Revealed below is my solution to the puzzle
that has garnered the attention of thousands worldwide.  Will we find the buried item?  Read and see.
The key to solving Part 4 is removal of all the unnecessary letters.  The
entire design concept of the screen and the system for solving the last of the
puzzle is a pun – this is about “Kut Ting” as much as it is about “King Tut.”

A SATELLITE DISH ?
Taking the answer as an anagram, a few come to mind --
The first might be “THIS ALL EDITS.”  Not helpful.  If we’re done
cutting and all we get is this picture, where’s whatever is buried?  
Another anagram is “I-HELL IS TASTED.”  If there is an information
hell – trying to gain knowledge from useless data, the satellite dish
can symbolize those data and the quest to solve KRYPTOS is certainly
a taste of “I-HELL.”  But like the previous anagram, it doesn’t
resolve our quest.
The correct anagram, I think, is “L SLATE HIDES IT.”  This is a good
one.  If “T IS YOUR POSITION” as said in the Morse Code, then a slate
a position “L” is certainly believable. But where is Position L?

And – wow! – a map of KRYPTOS reveals itself from its hiding place in
the 97 characters.  Note the screen shaded in green and the water jet
– clearly indicated as ‘WJET’ and shown here in blue. The petrified
tree trunk is “W” for wood.  The “T” – from “T IS YOUR POSITION” –
looking at the code – is at the end of “WJET” and the anagram for
“RIGHT” shares that “T” and is shown in green.  The other two T’s are
in the “WRONG” area.  
And there we have – plain as day – the location of the buried item. 
Right under the tile marked “L.”  Err – one of the tiles marked “L.” 
But which one?  Looking carefully, we see that the “L” near or under
the sculpture is “DUL” (dull) and at the other location you can form
the letters “BLOU” or “below you.”  The buried item is obviously
hidden under the tile marked with the second “L.” !
But wait.  Our quest may not be over.  The “SATELLITE DISH” could
mean not “THIS ALL EDITS” but “THIS ALL e DITS” – a clue, perhaps, as
to how to fill in the “e’s” (Morse dits) in the Morse code section. 
Solving the Morse Code could reveal that the map is wrong and there
is nothing buried.  Or perhaps both meanings – “L SLATE HIDES IT” and
“THIS ALL e DITS” are correct.
And so the mystery lives on!  The only way to know is to solve the
Morse Code.  And to DIG!

Taking look at the letters we cut out in (I) you will see
we have twelve letters:

KR YP AR CUT KKE
The “hot air” letters removed are:
TT SS QQ JS QQ SS BB DD FF KK LL OO UU
Eliminating the doubles from those we have fourteen
letters:
TSQJSQSBDFKLOU
All told, we have 26 letters, which is equal to the number
of “e” dits in the Morse Code and dovetails nicely with
the “This all e dits”  interpretation of “SATELLITE DISH”
above.  In other words, here are the 26 letters that need
to be used to complete the Morse Code section (N.B.
arriving at a count of 26 “e” dits requires the
interpretation of this Morse Code:
-..  ..  --.  .  -  .-  .-..  .  .  . 
to be read as “DIG e TAL eee” and not as “DIGETALeee.”).
Alternatively, we could assume that all of the double letters are
“hot air” in accordance with the text and simply drift away. 
Including the “out of place” letters from the encrypted text and
the tableau, we have:
YAR L KR YP AR CUT KKE 
and a “?”
which we can use to form a title for the piece.  There are plenty
of anagrams to be formed with these letters and the meaning here
is certainly subject to debate.  In some senses Mr. Sanborn could
asking us to interpret his work by choosing the right anagram.  I
like this idea that you have to get past a lot of hot air to find
a useful meaning in an artistic piece, and both this
interpretation and the use of the abscissa as “e” dits may be
correct.
You could say the remain letters form this:
KRYP CUT ARRAY KKEL
If you could turn the “L” into an “A” the LKKE becomes “KAKE” and
you have a place to put the “CANDLE” that we did not use in our
above solution.

How to turn the “L” into “A?”  Take “CENTRAL INTELLIGENCE AGENCY”
and begin with “T” as “YOUR POSITION.”  Make it through the “R A”
(ARRAY) but now you are in “L” (HELL).  You need to “step back”
into the array and find another way out (i.e., get the message).
But with an “L” turned into an “A” you could also have this:
Y KRY? KLEAR CUT PARK 
Which could be interpreted to mean be a criticism of clear cutting
of trees where KRYPTOS stands.  With this concept, you could say
the Central Intelligence Agency took a “step back”when it cut the
trees.
If you don’t like either of the “stepping back” idea, you could
instead say that from the raise letters on the screen you can pull
“Y?” and then you balance “AR” on one side with “L” on the other.
This suggest that AR=L and you can replace one with the other.
Which gives us:
Y? YLLKRYPARCUTKKE 
or
Y? TRY KRACK.  KEY: PULL
Which mean to understand the message you have to pull letters from
the screen to “krack” the puzzle.
These are the meaning I found here.  But you can come up with your
own interpretation.  That’s what art is all about.
