SO...
The scheme appears to be that, without looking further at K4,
two strings, DIAWI and INFBN end up being converted to DWAII 
and IBFNN, both of which end in doublets following this rule:
  a. The original string appears in K4 in its original K4
     shift (0-26), pos. 21-25. (DIAWI at shift 0, INFBN at 
     shift 5)
  b. The same string is then shifted to K4 shift (original   
     shift -5). (DIAWI 0(26) - 5 = 21, INFBN 5 - 5 = 0)
  c. A converted form of the same characters, but ending in a
     doublet of the two common characters appears shifted 
     again -5. (DWAII 21 - 5 = 16, IBFNN 0(26) - 5 = 21)
In other words, the original string appears in positions 21-25
of the third row of a K4 shift (0 through 25). It is then
shifted a -5 K4 shift and appears in positions 25-29 of the
third row. Finally, it appears after another -5 K4 shift in
positions 26-30 of the 2nd row after having undergone a 
transposition that reverses the values of the two similar 
characters (places the second character in the fourth position
and places the fourth character in the second position
according to the rule that appears in the third paragraph on
page 1.
Now, it can be shown that this scheme occurs for all shifts
of K4. IE, the string residing in the 3rd row, pos. 21-25,
OXOOX, will, after two shifts and a transposition of the values
in the Xes (2 and 4), will end up as OOOXX in Row 2, pos. 26-30.
There will be two K4 shifts of -5 each.
One has to ask, will the reverse occur if I take the ﬁrst 
doublet and preceding 3 characters of K4 shift 0, LIFBB, and
attempt to arrive at LBFIB by shifting +5 twice? The answer
is NO. In fact this procedure does not work for any doublet-
ended strings in K4 shift 0 except for the NGKSS string in the 
2nd row, pos. 26-30. Reversing back from NGKSS, we arrive
initially at K4 shift 10, pos. 21-25 (as we expected). 
How about K4 shift 16 where DWAII lies? Will BHLGG produce
BGLHG if we reverse the process? The answer is again NO. 
Nor will any 5-character strings ending in doublets in 
locations other than the 2nd row, pos. 26-30.

There is one odd occurrance where two strings exhibiting the
structure OXOOX are together and oﬀset from the other by one 
position. They are in K4 shift 0 starting at 3rd row, position
3. They are TQSJQ and QSJQS. These sort of stood out as I was
scanning everything looking for any cases where the OXOOX
situation existed. I could ﬁnd no relationship between them and
the other connections that I made.
So, The bottom line at this point seems to be that the characters
surrounding the CT implied to look for a way to complete Hoover’s
name. A shift of 21 was applied that resulted in the OOH
appearing. The positive result of this actually could have caused
me to look at the adjacent letters around OOH. Together, they
are QOOHUAKFR. However, I did a 21 shift of all of K4 and found
DIAWI in another place. This made me want to see if other shifts
got results. They did. A shift of 5 found INFBN, which I saw
merged with DIAWI in the 0 shift of K4. This confused me at ﬁrst,
as I was thinking that DIAWI and INFBN were related in a way 
other than the Caesar shift of 5. IE a shift of 21 is encode and
a shift of 5 is decode since 26 - 21 = 5. I now think INFBN was
chosen as as clue to use shifts of 5. This caused me to arrive
at a shift of 16 which had DWAII in it. I looked at the values
and rationalized how DWAII could come from DIAWI and then 
remembered the switching of the values of the I and I according 
to what I discovered about lining up WIDIAWIDIA/1234512345, 
getting DIAWI now worth 14325 instead of 12345. Now it made some 
sense.
Now, we have for all shifts of K4, An origin for a string in the
form of OXOOX lying in pos. 21-25 of the 3rd row of K4 shift Y, 
then a Y-5 K4 shift with OXOOX lying in pos. 25-29 of row 3, then
a ﬁnal -5 K4 shift (to Y-10) but with a transposition of 
characters to the form OOOXX in pos. 26-30 of the 2nd row. The 
regime is valid for all 26 K4 shifts. But this scheme is valid
only in the positions I mentioned above:
Y - 0  OXOOX positions 21-25 in Row 3
y - 5  OXOOX positions 25-29 in Row 3 (a K4 shift of -5)
y - 10 OOOXX positions 26-30 in Row 2 (a K4 shift of -5)
I don’t feel that DIAWI or INFBN are speciﬁcally pertinent. I
think he used them to map out the shifts. Interesting that he
identiﬁed them as relating to Hoover, which conﬁrmed that
solving for OOH on the outset was essentially a necessary step
in moving forward.

What to consider as the next step? After manipulating with
two shifts and a transposition resulting in strings ending
in doublets, I would have thought that keeping the doublets 
out of the resulting substitution routine would be prudent. 
But we don’t know what K4 CT says except for NYPVTTMZFPK says
BERLINCLOCK. And the TT corresponds with the SS in ABSCISS
(since a Z has replaced the ? at the front of K4 CT).
Three things are going on that show up involving shifts of 5.
1. Out of all 26 shifts of K4 (0 through 25), ﬁve positive
   shifts have been chosen as original positions:
   a. The original position of DIAWI is at shift 0.
   b. The original position of INFBN is at shift 0 + 5 = 5.
   c. The original position of NSKGS is at shift 5 + 5 = 10.
2. Out of all 26 shifts of K4 (0 through 25), SHIFTS OF -5
   from original positions have been chosen as
   intermediate positions (numbers below are shifts):
   a. The intermediate position of DIAWI is at a shift
      of -5 from the original position 0(=26), 26 - 5 = 21.
   b. The intermediate position of INFBN is at shift of
      -5 from the original position 5, 5 - 5 = 0.
   c. The intermediate position of NSKGS is at a shift
      of -5 from the original position 10, 10 - 5 = 5.
3. Out of all 26 shifts of K4 (0 through 25), SHIFTS OF -5
   from intermediate positions have been chosen
   as destination positions and their strings have undergone 
   transpositions from 12345 to 14325.
   a. The destination position of DIAWI and transposition to
      DWAII is at a shift of -5 from the intermediate
      position of 21, 21 - 5 = 16.
   b. The destination position of INFBN and transposition to
      IBFNN is at a shift of -5 from the intermediate 
      position of 0(=26), 26 - 5 = 21.
   c. The destination position of NSKGS and transposition to
      NGKSS is at a shift of -5 from the intermediate
      position of 5, 5 - 5 = 0.
