Part 2: The Triple-Layer Berlin Clock Shift 
The Berlin Clock (Mengenlehreuhr) is a set-theory clock. The shift key for each letter is not static but 
changes based on three clock variables. 
The Shift Formula 
Shift = (H5 + H1) + (M5 + M1) +- Sblink 
• 
Row 1 & 2 (Hours): Provide the Base Shift. 
• 
Row 3 (5-Minute Blocks): The Red Lights (at the 3rd, 6th, and 9th positions) trigger a shift

Blinking Light (2 Seconds): The Fine-Tune shift of +- 1 or +-2 
The formula tells you how the pieces move (the shifts). But it doesn’t tell you us the starting position 
of the pieces. This explains why computers couldn't solve it (because the key blinks and moves).  
 
Part 3: The Time-Sweep Calibration 
Identifying the Zero Hour (The Anchor) 
The word BERLIN points us to a specific historical Zero Hour: 06:52 PM, the moment the Berlin 
Wall’s opening was announced. 
Synchronizing the Blink (S) 
The clock has a top light that blinks every 2 seconds. In Step 3, you have to decide if a specific letter 
was encoded on an on blink or an off blink. This is why you adjust our results by +- 1 or +- 2. 
• 
Without the blink adjustment: You get gibberish like LOCASD. 
• 
With the blink adjustment: It snaps into the perfect word LOCATED. 
The Running Key Sweep 
Because K4 is a Moving Clock cipher, the key doesn't stay the same for the whole message. As you 
move from the first letter to the 97th, the clock in the code is ticking. Step 3 involves crawling 
through the text and updating the shift value every time the 14-column grid moves to a new row.
