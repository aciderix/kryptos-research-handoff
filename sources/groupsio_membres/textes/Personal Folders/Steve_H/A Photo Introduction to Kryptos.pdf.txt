A Photo Introduction to Kryptos 
This is a quick introduction to the Kryptos encrypted sculpture at CIA headquarters in Virginia.  
It is a guide to the pieces that make up Kryptos and it's history. 
There is some information about the parts already solved at the end. 
 
 
 
  
 
What is Kryptos 
Kryptos is an encrypted sculpture by American artist Jim Sanborn located on the grounds of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) in Langley, 
Virginia. When the CIA was constructing a new headquarters building behind its original headquarters they wanted an outdoor art installation 
for the area between the two buildings. Since its dedication on November 3, 1990, there has been much speculation about the meaning of the 
encrypted messages it bears. Of the four messages (known as K1, K2, K3 and K4), three have been solved, with the fourth remaining one of the 
most famous unsolved codes in the world.  
The name Kryptos comes from the Greek word for "hidden", and the theme of the sculpture is "intelligence gathering." The most prominent feature 
is a large vertical S-shaped copper screen resembling a scroll, or piece of paper emerging from a computer printer, covered with characters 
constituting encrypted text. The characters consist of the 26 letters of the standard Latin alphabet and question marks cut out of the copper. 
The sculpture gives the appearance there was once something else written underneath, then wiped away, and the new text written, a "palimpsest". 
A palimpsest is a manuscript page, either from a scroll or a book, from which the text has been either scraped or washed off so that the page 
can be used again. Palimpsest was a keyword used to solve K1. 
 
There is more to Kryptos than just the sculpture 
At the same time as the main sculpture was installed, sculptor Jim Sanborn also placed several other pieces around CIA grounds, such as several 
large granite slabs with sandwiched copper sheets outside the entrance to the New Headquarters Building. Several morse code messages are 
engraved in the copper, and one of the slabs has an engraved compass rose and a lodestone. Other elements of Sanborn's installation include a 
landscaped area, a fishk pond, a reflecting pool, and several other seemingly unmarked slabs. There are some elements around CIA grounds the 
public has not seen. So far only the sculpture itself was needed to solve the first 3 parts. So far only Jim Sanborn knows the answer to K4.  
On the front lawn in front of the entrance to the New HQ Building there are:  Morse code on slabs, a lodestone, an engraved compass pointing to 
the lodestone and a pyramid shaped slab. 
In the courtyard there are: The S shaped sculpture with petrified tree, whirlpool, fish pond, slabs with no apparant markings, 3 benches and a 
large letter J formed from plants that cuts into the restuarant building. Jim Sanborn designed only the semi circle landscaped area and it's 
features and the S shaped sculpture and pool and the boulder, slate, slabs.  He did not design the zig zag walls or other parts of the 
courtyard. 
Jim Sanborn said you should look at the art installation as if you were flying over it. A copy of Jim Sanborns letter to CIA employees 
describing the installation is on the last page.

As shown in the photo the compass will not point North, it will always point to the lodestone.  A lodestone, or loadstone, is a naturally 
magnetized piece of the mineral magnetite. They are naturally-occurring magnets, which can attract iron. The property of magnetism was first 
discovered through lodestones. Pieces of lodestone, suspended so they could turn, were the first magnetic compasses, and their importance to 
early navigation is indicated by the name lodestone, which in Middle English means 'course stone' or 'leading stone', from the now-obsolete 
meaning of lode as ‘journey, way'. Lodestone is one of only two minerals that is found naturally magnetized; the other is pyrrhotite.  
Lodestones were once believed to possess magical properties of various kinds. If one was ill, the stone should be held in the hands and shaken 
well. It was said to cure wounds, snakebites, weak eyes, headaches, and defective hearing. The possessor of the lodestone was supposed to be 
able to walk through reptiles in safety even when they were accompanied by "black death." Orpheus stated that "with this stone you can hear the 
voices of the gods and learn many wonderful things," that it had the property of unfolding the future, and if held close to the eyes, it would 
inspire with a divine spirit.

The Berlin Wall 
Jim Sanborn was given the commission to create Kryptos in 1988. It was formally dedicated on November 3, 1990. In between the Berlin Wall was 
first breached on 9 November 1989 and the wall came down. Jim Sanborn has said he was influenced by the wall coming down just like other people 
were but has never hinted that K4 is connected to the Berlin Wall. In November 2010 Jim Sanborn released a clue stating the letters NYPVTT in 
K4 were BERLIN (or NILREB).  This has added to speculation that K4 may be connected to the Berlin Wall. 
On CIA grounds there is a monument to the Berlin Wall, an actual 3 part section of the wall from Berlin.  It is situated in the middle of a 
path. It was dedicated in December 1992. It is not part of the Kryptos art installation.  A bronze plaque near the Wall reads, "These three 
sections of reinforced concrete were removed from the Berlin Wall near Checkpoint Charlie at Potsdamer Platz in November 1989."

Kryptos K1 to K4 and the Morse Code and how K1, K2 and K3 were solved 
 
After Jim Sanborn decided he wanted to incorporate some encrypted messages in his artwork, he was teamed with Ed Scheidt, who was in the 
process of retiring as the Chairman of the Central Intelligence Agency Cryptographic Center . Up until that point, Jim Sanborn had never used 
encryption or text in his work. Ed Scheidt taught various encryption methods to Jim Sanborn, who chose the exact messages to be encrypted. The 
solutions to Kryptos were placed in a sealed envelope and handed to William Webster who was at the time the Director of the Central 
Intelligence Agency. They were never checked by anyone other than Jim Sanborn. 
The encryption was all done by hand by Jim Sanborn using paper and pencil and the concept was for a CIA field operative returning home to CIA 
to use just paper and a pencil to solve Kryptos. The encryption was completed while on a trip to retrieve the petrified wood for the sculpture. 
A photo of the original encoding charts for K1, K2 and K3 can be found at the end of this file.  In the early days Jim Sanborn said K1, K2 and 
K3 could be solved by a field operative using pen and paper in a couple of days and K4 would take a little longer. Jim Sanborn has said that 
you do not need to solve K1, K2 and K3 to solve K4 but has also said there are clues in K1, K2 and K3 to solve K4.  He has said all clues are 
in plain sight. Ed Scheidt said "don't hold your breath" when referring to K4, possibly the answer is in one of the ponds. 
California computer scientist Jim Gillogly was the first person in 1999 to announce the solutions to K1, K2 and K3. He used a Pentium II 
computer to do it. Then came an announcement (CIA had kept it secret) that a year earlier CIA analyst David Stein decrypted K1, K2 and K3 after 
spending some 400 hours worth of lunch hours working through the puzzles using only paper and pencil. Then came an announcement that the 
National Security Agency (NSA) had solved the first 3 parts in 1992. More than 20 years later no one has published the solution to K4.

A simple way to describe classical ciphers (used by K1 to K3) 
You either use a scheme to change the actual letters or use a scheme to change where they are in the text. 
"Substitution" ciphers substitute the letters in the alphabet for other letters.   One version of this is to reverse the alphabet, so that when 
you want to write ABCDE you instead write ZYXWV.  
"Transposition" ciphers do not change any letters in the message, they re-arrange them in a format that can be hard to work out if you don't 
know how. ABCDE could be DACBE and it could also be EABCD.   
 
K1 
The first 63 letters in the top left box are K1.  They are " EMUFPHZLRFAXYUSDJKZLDKRNSHGNFIVJYQTQUXQBQVYUVLLTREVJYQTMKYRDMFD " 
K1 uses a modified Vigenère cipher. It's encrypted through substitution — each letter corresponds to another— and can be solved only with the 
alphabetic rows of letters on the right on the Tableau side of the sculpture. The keywords, which help determine the substitutions, are KRYPTOS 
and PALIMPSEST. A misspelling — in this case IQLUSION — may be a clue to cracking K4. 
K1 solution (13 words): 
BETWEEN SUBTLE SHADING AND THE ABSENCE OF LIGHT LIES THE NUANCE OF IQLUSION

A different technique was used for K3: transposition. All the letters are jumbled and can be deciphered only by uncovering the complex matrices 
and mathematics that determined their misplacement. Of course, there is a misspelling (DESPARATLY), and the last sentence (CAN YOU SEE 
ANYTHING?) is strangely bracketed by an X and a Q.

K3 is a paraphrased quotation from Howard Carter's account of the opening of the tomb of Tutankhamun on November 26, 1922, as described in his 
1923 book The Tomb of Tutankhamun. The question with which it ends is that posed by Lord Carnarvon, to which Carter (in the book) famously 
replied "wonderful things". In the actual November 26, 1922 field notes, his reply was, "Yes, it is wonderful.". The Tomb of Tutankhamun was 
one of Jim Sanborn's favourite books and he has said that solving Kryptos could be similar to entering the tomb, with one doorway leading to 
another.

No solution has ever been published. It is not known if the CIA or NSA have solved it.  They never revealed they had solved the first 3 parts 
until the solutions were made public.  Many Freedom of Information requests have been made to CIA and NSA and many documents received in 
response but never the answer to K4.  NSA have stated that when they looked at K4 and decided what type of encoding was most likely to have 
been used they decided not to proceed.  That's the official story. 
One reason that K4 has proven difficult to crack is that according to Jim Sanborn and Ed Scheidt a masking technique was used. In addition to 
obtaining a ciphertext for K4 Jim Sanborn used ways to hide what that the plaintext or ciphertext should look like.  This had made it 
impossible, as far as we know, for even computers to solve it. Masking hides the ciphertext.  Removing all vowels could be used, deleting 
letters, changing every nth letter to the one before it in the alphabet are all forms of masking.  There are many ways to mask the plaintext 
and the ciphertext. Ed Scheidt said to crack K4 you need to work out the techniques used first.
