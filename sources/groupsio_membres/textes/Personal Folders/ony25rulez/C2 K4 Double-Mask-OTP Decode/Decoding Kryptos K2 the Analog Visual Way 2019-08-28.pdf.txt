All the repeating sections appear to be at a distance as a multiple of 8. Repeating segments of DQM and TBJ are the 
smallest distances apart (8 characters) and very likely this would be the periodicity of the cipher. Moreover, the 
countless number of repeating segments would support some form of a substitution cipher rather than a 
transposition because the position of the plaintext and cipher text appears to be maintained and the number of 
repeating segments identified. 
Stenographic Clues in Kryptos models 
K1 utilized the top half of the KRYPTOS tableau to provide the plaintext order (i.e. KRYPTOSABC…) for the Quagmire 
III decode. Using the same pattern of stenography, it is very difficult to conclude exactly which portions of the tableau 
is used on the CIA sculpture. That being said, if Jim Sanborn intended on the use of stenography on the tableau, then 
the information would be preserved in other smaller scaled Kryptos replicas. 
Mini-Kryptos, 2005 Jim Sanborn dinner models (larger) and the studio tabletop Kryptos all indicate the L and Z 
portion of the bottom tableau to be of significance (see below). 
From L to Z on the tableau, it is 9 lines or a 1+8 format. That is, the tableau is the 9 lines with the first being the plain 
alphabet character ordering and the subsequent 8 being the actual cipher alphabet (by rows).

For rows 1 through 8 there would be a Caesar shift by “x” units. While it may seem difficult to determine the plaintext 
from the tableau manually, the symmetrical nature of classical substitution is the secret to encoding and decoding. 
That is, the encoding and decoding is done by the exact same process of utilizing the tableau. If certain relationships 
can be identified, then the plaintext can be predicted to produce the decoded message. 
Similar to the K1 Quagmire III decode, the most important weakness of a Vigenère style tableau is that the 
polyalphabetic substitution is on a 1:1 basis. For each row, K plaintext can only be encoded to a single character (A-
Z) and the character shifts between the plaintext or ciphertext rows must be maintained. As an example, the number 
of shifts from K to B is 8 (K to RYPTOSAB). This is identical on the ciphertext rows regardless of the offset. 
For the purposes of the manual method, the order of HIJL…EFG of the plaintext ordering will be maintained to match 
the process of K1. This might be useful to provide some clue for K4 in the future. The next step is to mark down all 
the letters used by the ciphertext:

Discussion and Analysis – Comparison to K1 
Plaintext Alphabet Alignment 
Similar to K1, K2 was decoded from a substitution Vigenere style cipher that exploited the symmetrical nature of the 
encryption and decryption. Both ciphertext and plaintext utilize the identical key of ABSCISSA via Quagmire III 
cipher. To match the original tableau, it may be of importance to maintain the alignment of the plaintext alphabet 
where KRYPTOS is in the middle of the tableau rather than at the beginning like Quagmire III. 
IDBYROWS vs XLAYERTWO 
The final 8 characters yields IDBYROWS which may or may not have been intentional due to Jim Sanborn’s K2 
correction. Following Jim’s “onion-layer” theory, it is possible that Jim intentionally omitted the X for the decryption 
as both a clue and a checkpoint. In terms of a clue, the IDBYROWS might be prompt for the K3 transposition while 
Jim may have intentionally withheld the XLAYERTWO until he felt the Kryptos community reached a certain stage in 
the decryption. 
Kryptos Tableau 
By identifying the tableau sections for K1 and K2, there are two sections in the tableau that have yet to be used. It 
may be possible that there is information to deal with K3 and K4 but may have to be explored in the future. 
 
K4 Predictions 
Similar to the K1 manual decrypt for substitutions, K4 could be much more difficult if the substitution attacks are 
mitigated by an non-cyclical asymmetrical key without the use of plain English. Combined with a transposition 
cipher, it would be extremely difficult to decrypt. As mentioned in the C2 K4-Double-Mask-OTP hypothesis (July 15, 
2019), a possible decryption process would be the transposition of K4 characters and then the decryption via one-
time pad (Vernam, XOR, etc.). An unorthodox transposition would be best for Jim Sanborn to leverage his creativity.
