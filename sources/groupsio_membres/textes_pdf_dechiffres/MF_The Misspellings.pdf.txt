The Misspellings 
 
CNN: Why did you put deliberate misspellings in some of the clues? 
Sanborn: That's an ancient tool for making codes more difficult to crack.  Saying I was sloppy is a good way of saying it.  I didn't take tremendous care 
in what's been deciphered already, because it was -- if you want to say it's intentional, it's intentional.  Because it makes it harder.  It's unpredictable.  
Unpredictability is one of the strengths of difficult code, and I wanted to be as unpredictable as possible. 
 
Sanborn said that Scheidt had told him that misspellings could be good, since they made things harder to solve. 
 
Sanborn said that the misspellings were deliberate, but it was less important what they were, than the orientation of those letters.  Later on in the 
evening, he repeated the point, saying that it was the positioning that was important.  (Elonka’s Roadtrip)

The second word in K3’s PT, DESPARATLY, provides a conundrum. 
 
First off, Sanborn would not confirm that DESPARATLY was an intentional spelling error.  (Elonka’s Roadtrip) 
 
Further, if you leave room for any amount of spelling errors then obviously DESPARATLY could become any word. 
However, there are at least four words that are similar enough to DESPARATLY that they could result from a minimal collection of spelling errors. 
 
The first two, DESPERATELY and DISPARATELY, involve two spelling errors: an incorrect letter and a dropped letter.
